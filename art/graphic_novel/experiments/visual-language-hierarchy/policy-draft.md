# Layered Visual Authority - Experimental Policy Draft

Status: branch-local experiment. This document does not replace `SOP.md` or `style_guide.md` unless separately reviewed and adopted.

## Authority Hierarchy

Apply visual authorities in this order:

1. **Canon and character identity** govern what exists, who is present, established physical traits, chronology, technology, and narrative facts.
2. **Chapter 1 foundational grammar** governs rendering discipline, restrained linework, credible anatomy, material specificity, coherent perspective, physical plausibility, and production finish.
3. **Contextual authorities** govern variable qualities when accepted references directly match the location, era, character state, or scene function. These qualities include palette, weather, lighting, texture density, camera language, contrast, and panel rhythm.
4. **Scene-specific adaptation** may depart from earlier treatments when the change is motivated by chronology, environment, narrative state, or function and does not override a higher authority.

When authorities conflict, canon and identity govern content; foundational grammar governs craft; contextual references govern atmosphere and local cinematography. New treatments remain experimental until compared and approved.

## Recommended Production Wording

`chapter_01_wakeup/continuity-sheet.png` establishes the project's foundational rendering grammar: linework restraint, anatomical credibility, material specificity, perspective coherence, physical plausibility, and production finish. It does not impose a universal palette, lighting scheme, contrast structure, texture density, camera language, or panel rhythm. For each generation, accepted references directly relevant to location, era, character state, and scene function may act as contextual authorities for those variable qualities. Scene-specific departures must be traceable to manuscript context and may not override canon, character identity, physical credibility, or foundational finish. When authorities conflict, canon and identity govern content; foundational grammar governs craft; contextual references govern atmosphere and local cinematography. New treatments remain experimental until compared and approved.

## Guardrails

- Contextual variation must be traceable to an accepted reference or manuscript condition.
- Identity, anatomy, materials, perspective, and finish must remain stable across regimes.
- Contextual authority is not permission for arbitrary novelty, stylistic imitation, or reduced finish.
- Do not introduce canon, insignia, technology, architecture, or character traits absent from approved sources.
- Reject neon cyberpunk drift, superhero gloss, fantasy styling, illegible action, or atmosphere that destroys physical readability.
- Accepted art remains unchanged until a replacement is separately approved.

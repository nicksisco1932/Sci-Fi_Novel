# Voss Conquest River-City Study

Status: adaptation-only concept art. The city, district geometry, architecture, clothing, and administrative fixtures in this set are production designs, not manuscript or lore canon.

## Chosen Location

The study uses an unnamed Minority World-State river city centered on a civic bridge, a transit-and-civic exchange, and an attached public-clinic records wing. The location was selected because the Voss Conquest appendix makes urban destruction legible through transport, clinics, household rolls, rationing, siege logistics, and administrative control without naming a specific city.

The production terms `river city`, `exchange`, and `clinic-records room` are descriptive labels only, not proper nouns.

## Nested Scale Relationship

- **Macro:** the river, civic bridge and rail, exchange hall, clinic wing, quays, utilities, and surrounding districts.
- **Meso:** the same bridgehead from above the river, preserving the exchange canopy and the clinic as a long river-parallel block with river-facing windows, repeated sawtooth roof bays, and one circular service element at its bridge-facing end.
- **Micro:** one clinic intake room behind those river-facing windows, preserving the window, tile band, shelving, ceiling conduit and lamp, examination couch, desk, and basin.

## Temporal Change

### Before

- `before-macro.png`: functioning citywide transport, river traffic, maintained utilities, ordinary density, and modest repair.
- `before-meso.png`: open exchange circulation, clinic access, freight, maintenance, market activity, and daily travel.
- `before-micro.png`: an ordinary clinic intake and records room in active civic use.

### During

- `during-macro.png`: stalled transit, requisition convoys, displaced queues, clinic overflow, shuttering, and scattered utility failure.
- `during-meso.png`: temporary processing lines, controlled bridge movement, commandeered exchange space, halted rail, rationing, and triage awnings.
- `during-micro.png`: the clinic room divided between care, household screening, and medical-supply requisition.

### After

- `after-macro.png`: restored but sparse transit, standardized control kiosks, narrowed routes, regulated river use, and cold repairs over older fabric.
- `after-meso.png`: permanent inspection infrastructure, a largely emptied plaza, bounded public movement, and the clinic under durable administrative control.
- `after-micro.png`: the former clinic room used for identity clearance and labor allocation while the couch, basin, medical containers, tile, and repair scars remain visible.

## Constants

The river bend, bridge arches and rail, exchange canopy, clinic massing and orientation, bridge-facing circular service element, sawtooth roof, river-facing windows, quays, and major skyline remain recognizable across the set. The micro triptych retains the same room camera and fixed interior anchors.

## Historical Logic

The study uses the Thirty Years' War as a model for attrition, scarcity, hollowed density, civilian exhaustion, and institutions wearing down over time. It uses World War II occupation logic for checkpoints, registries, requisition, transport control, bureaucracy, medical overflow, and reconstruction layered over trauma. No specific uniform, flag, insignia, real battle image, or famous composition is imitated.

Violence is carried through absence, damaged services, queues, controlled movement, confiscated supplies, repurposed rooms, and the reduction of civic life rather than graphic slaughter.

The Surface Age stack, Hive city, Lower Grid, clinic, market, and checkpoint references were inspected for scale, repair logic, and readability but were not used to establish the city's architecture. This avoids importing later Hive design or the provisional Surface Age silo into the Voss-era city.

## Prompt Summaries

All nine prompts required portrait `1024x1536`, sophisticated black-and-white or restrained grayscale line art, fine controlled ink contours, disciplined hatching, readable perspective and silhouettes, no lettering, no symbols, no gore, no copied historical imagery, and no muddy concept-art drift.

- BEFORE prompts established the unchanged city, bridgehead, and clinic-room geometry through ordinary public use.
- DURING prompts edited the matching BEFORE views with temporary barriers, rationing, requisition, triage, screening, service failures, and displaced civilians while locking camera and architecture.
- AFTER prompts returned to the BEFORE geometry and used DURING only as historical residue, replacing temporary takeover with sparse restored transit, permanent control systems, constrained movement, and repurposed civic space.

## Development Use

The set can support future decisions about conquest-era urban logistics, civilian survival, occupation design, and the material ancestry of later memory traditions. None of its new visual details should be treated as canon unless separately reviewed and promoted.

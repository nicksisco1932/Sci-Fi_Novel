## Archive Note

**Human Continuity Archive, 5670 CE**

**Voice-Dictated Entry: Surface Age / Para-Ancient Conflict Register**

These accounts belong to the last centuries of surface civilization, before the [[Hive Spindle World Document|Hive]] orders made continuity possible. The record is incomplete. Most surviving histories were preserved by the Coalition of the Willing.

Where certainty is not possible, this appendix records the shape of the dispute.

## The Voss Conquest

The Voss Conquest is the later name for the post-assassination destruction of the Minority World-State by the States of the Great Angel. Contemporary records did not call it conquest. Coalition summaries called it stabilization, correction, and preservation. Rebel accounts called it extermination.

The rebel term is not neutral. It is also harder to dismiss.

The war began after [[Appendix – Figures of the Turning Time#Maribel Voss|Maribel Voss]] was dead. That fact matters less than it should. Her assassination gave the factions something they had lacked while she lived: a corpse that could not correct them. Voss had taught them the language of scale, necessity, and civilizational survival. After her death, that language moved without her.

The Great Union did not collapse in a single break. It failed the way overlarge systems often fail: jurisdiction by jurisdiction, exception by exception, office by office. Emergency councils became provisional governments. Provisional governments became armed claims. Armed claims became states with flags, records, and prisons.

The States of the Great Angel formed out of this breakage. They were not, at first, a clean bloc. They were army commands, preservation offices, loyalist ministries, and emergency courts that discovered they could survive by agreeing on an enemy.

The Minority World-State remains difficult to define from surviving records. It was a political formation, a protected minority system, and a population category. It may also have been the last legal shelter for people marked as incompatible with Great Union continuity. The uncertainty is not an accident. Many of the records that would have clarified it were destroyed by the people who benefited from the confusion.

The early phase resembled a civil war with too many sponsors. Armies crossed borders that had stopped meaning what maps claimed. Commanders changed patrons. Cities paid one side for protection and another side not to burn them. Grain levies became military policy. Disease followed the armies because the armies carried hunger with them.

The middle phase became worse. The States of the Great Angel learned to describe civilian survival as enemy capacity. A village with food became a supply base. A clinic became a restoration cell. A school became a future command structure. Once that logic took hold, restraint became evidence of disloyalty.

Siege was the preferred instrument because siege let murder look administrative. Water was cut. Rations were priced beyond reach. Medical corridors were opened late or not at all. When cities surrendered, the rolls were checked before the gates were opened.

The phrase "to the man, woman, and child" appears first in hostile accounts. Great Angel records used softer terms: household clearance, line closure, and demographic correction. The archive preserves both vocabularies because the difference is part of the crime.

No reliable count survives. The numbers are too damaged by propaganda, missing registries, and later correction campaigns. The scale is clearer than the count. Whole districts disappear from tax records. River provinces lose names from maps. Agricultural zones show harvest gaps that last longer than a season can explain.

Proto-Under Hive murals preserve the rivers as red. Coalition summaries call this exaggeration. Surviving intake closures, disposal orders, and fishery quarantines make the denial narrow.

The religious character of the war is disputed because the surface age had already learned to launder belief through policy. The Great Angel did not need every soldier to pray. It needed them to accept that the enemy stood outside the future. That was enough. Once a population was placed outside continuity, slaughter could be filed as maintenance.

The final phase was not merely military. It was clerical, legal, and logistical. Census offices identified family lines. Security courts converted suspicion into sentence. Transport authorities moved survivors away from witnesses. The extermination did not replace administration. It used administration.

This is why later histories disagree so sharply over whether the Voss Conquest was a war, a purge, or a failed consolidation. The categories all apply. It had battles, but battles do not explain it. It had ideology, but ideology does not account for the invoices. It had grief, but grief became useful too quickly.

The Coalition of the Willing preserved the dominant record because it survived near the winning side. Its archives are extensive and compromised. They include atrocity denials, casualty ledgers, tribunal fragments, and private memoranda that say more than their authors intended.

Rebel accounts are less complete and often more accurate in their hatred. They preserve names the official records converted into numbers. They also preserve stories that cannot be verified: red rivers, sealed wells, children hidden in drainage works, whole towns answering roll call after death because clerks refused to mark them gone.

The Conquest did not end cleanly. It exhausted itself into treaties, annexations, and administrative silence. The Minority World-State was destroyed as a political shelter. Some of its people survived by changing names, entering labor blocs, or disappearing below the surface. Later Proto-Under Hive traditions treat those survivals as the beginning of a duty: remember what the surface learned to make forgettable.

The Hive orders did not descend from the Voss Conquest alone. That would be too simple. But the Conquest left behind a lesson that later systems never fully rejected: fragmentation can kill a civilization, and unity can learn to kill in civilization's name.

By [[Cassian Rho]]'s era, the Voss Conquest is old enough to be mural history, archive dispute, and inherited insult. Its practical use remains intact. When a faction wants to remove a people and still call itself necessary, it reaches for language first. The killing follows after.

The dark beyond the red line held for three breaths.

Then it answered.

One knock from deeper in the corridor.

Two back from somewhere above [[Cassian Rho|Cassian]]'s left shoulder.

A third from farther in, lighter, almost cautious.

Not random.

Not echo.

People.

Thena did not look around. That, more than anything, told him she understood the rules already forming.

"Hands visible," she said quietly.

"Agreed."

They stood in the corridor mouth with the med case between them, both hands clear, weapons still in reach but no longer pretending to be hidden.

The silence changed shape.

He felt the watchers before he saw them.

A narrow panel in the right-hand wall slid back with almost no sound. Dim amber light touched one section of corridor and left the rest alone. Beyond it, an eye studied them and vanished again.

A voice came from the dark ahead. Old enough to have learned patience properly. Gender hard to place.

"Step to the line and stop."

Cassian and Thena stepped once.

Stopped.

"Set the case down."

Cassian did.

"Kick it forward."

He nudged it across the floor until it crossed into shadow.

Something moved there.

Not rushed. Not fearful. Competent.

The case opened. Metal touched metal. A hinge clicked shut.

The voice came again.

"Who marked you through?"

Thena answered first. "We followed the cuts from the ladder."

"That was not the question."

Cassian said, "No one marked us by hand."

The pause that followed was longer this time.

"Then how did you find the descent?"

"We were moved," Cassian said.

Another shift in the dark.

Not alarm exactly.

Attention.

Thena added, "Before you ask, we know how that sounds."

"Good," the voice said. "Then you also know why it sounds like a lie."

From the wall to Cassian's right, another panel opened. This one wider. A compact weapon protruded first, handmade but well kept, its barrel wrapped against reflection. Behind it stood a narrow figure in layered fabric and scavenged plating, face mostly covered, stance balanced and unsentimental.

Not [[Hive Spindle World Document|Hive]].

Not amateur.

Two more shapes resolved deeper in the corridor.

One high.

One low.

Crossfire set before conversation.

Thena exhaled once through her nose.

"Better discipline than the clinic."

"That is a low standard," the masked figure said.

The first voice belonged to someone still out of sight.

"Turn out your wrists."

They did.

"Sleeves."

Cassian pulled his back first. Thena followed with her good hand. The watchers took their time inspecting exposed skin, bandage edges, old puncture marks, anything that might hide a tag or tracer. Human inspection. Slow because it had to be.

"The hand," the voice said.

Thena did not move.

"It's clean now."

"Show it anyway."

She looked at Cassian once, then unwound enough of the fresh bandage to expose the inner wrap. No filament. No glint. Just swollen flesh and properly sealed damage.

The masked figure with the weapon lowered it by half an inch.

Not trust.

Revision.

"You cut something out," the unseen voice said.

"A tracker in the clinic bandage," Cassian said.

"How did you find it?"

He could have answered a dozen ways.

None of them improved the room.

"We were told where to look."

The weapon came back up.

Thena closed her eyes briefly. "Cassian."

"It was still true," he said.

The first voice shifted position then, and its owner stepped finally into the low amber spill from the wall panel. Thin build. Gray at the temples. No insignia. No wasted movement. One hand empty. The other resting near a blade worn for use rather than threat.

They looked at Cassian first.

Then at Thena.

Then at the med case now sitting open in shadow.

"By who?" they asked.

"We don't know," Thena said.

"A terminal knows too much," Cassian said at the same time.

The older figure considered that without visible reaction.

"Those are not the same answer."

"They're the same problem," Thena said.

That earned the faintest change around the eyes.

Maybe humor.

Maybe fatigue.

The older figure crouched by the med case and sorted its contents quickly with practiced hands. Bandages. Sealant. Water. Blade. No beacon. No obvious injection trap. When they finished, they shut the lid and stood.

"If the systems walked you here," they said, "either someone spent a great deal to keep you alive, or someone wants to know where our doors are."

"We considered both," Cassian said.

"And still crossed the line."

"There was no better option."

"There usually isn't."

The masked watcher to the right finally spoke.

"Search them."

No one argued.

Two more figures emerged. Quick hands. Efficient work. They took Cassian's blade, checked his boots, waistband, sleeves, collar seams. They did the same to Thena with no apology and no indecency. One found the remaining clinic bandage scraps in a side pocket and burned them on the spot in a small metal cup.

The smell turned bitter and chemical.

Everyone watched the smoke.

When nothing signaled back, the older figure nodded once.

"All right."

Not welcome.

Not safety.

Just the next threshold.

They looked at Thena. "You're from above-lane routes."

"Yes."

"But not from here."

"No."

Their gaze moved to Cassian.

"And you are the thing the upper sectors are shredding themselves over."

Cassian did not answer that.

The older figure did not press.

"Fine," they said. "You'll answer to someone with more patience for mysteries than I have."

Thena glanced toward the dark behind them. "That's reassuring."

"It should not be."

The masked watcher gestured with the weapon.

"Move."

They were taken deeper in, not roughly but with enough control to make resistance idiotic.

The corridor widened after two turns into a chamber hidden behind the false wall of the buried street. The architecture changed immediately. Not civic fiction anymore. Survival, layered over ruins.

Hand-strung lights.

Water catchments built from cut pipe and welded sheet.

Sleeping rolls tucked into service alcoves.

Storage crates marked in chalk.

A cook surface gone cold but not long cold.

People pretending not to stare and failing.

Cassian catalogued without turning his head.

At least a dozen present.

More behind secondary partitions, if the sound carried clean.

No uplink glow.

No ambient Hive smoothing of voice or light.

Everything had to be done by hand, which meant everything cost someone time.

Thena saw it too.

Her shoulders changed a fraction.

Not relaxed.

Reoriented.

"You really live down here," she said.

The older figure gave her a flat look.

"Did you think the line was decorative?"

They passed a wall where old transit maps had been stripped and rewritten into columns of practical notation: water levels, safe corridors, blocked cuts, shift signals, watch rotations. Someone had drawn a thick border around one section and written:

DO NOT TRUST MOVING LIGHT

Under that, in a different hand:

UNLESS IT SAVES YOU FIRST

Cassian's eyes lingered a fraction too long.

The older figure noticed.

"You know that line," they said.

"I've met the exception."

"Unfortunate."

That answer was too quick to be improvised.

It told him more than a lecture would have.

This place knew of the hidden contact.

It did not necessarily like it.

They were brought at last to a room that had once been some kind of service office and was now stripped to essentials: one table, six chairs, a wall cabinet, a lantern, two exits, and no soft corners.

An interrogation room built by people who could not afford waste.

"Sit," the older figure said.

This time Cassian did.

Thena sat beside him, med case at her feet, injured hand on the table where everyone could see it.

The older figure remained standing.

"Start with the part that matters most," they said. "Why did the dead systems spend themselves on you?"

Thena looked at Cassian.

Cassian looked back at the older figure.

He could lie.

He would.

But down here, a useful lie had to survive human hearing, not machine parsing.

"Because something followed me forward," he said.

The room did not move.

That was the clearest sign yet that he finally had their attention.

"Forward from where?" the older figure asked.

"From before," Cassian said.

Thena muttered, barely audible, "You really do know how to make things worse."

The older figure ignored her.

"And what does this something want?"

Cassian thought of the cockpit.

The wave.

The voice that had not sounded like voice.

Stop the expansion.

"I don't know yet," he said. "But I know someone below your line thinks I need to stay alive long enough to remember it properly."

The silence that followed was different from the one at the boundary.

Less suspicious.

More dangerous.

Because now the wrong answer was no longer the only concern.

From the second exit came a soft double knock.

The older figure turned their head.

Waited.

Listened.

Then they looked back at Cassian with something colder and more specific in their face.

"You have timing," they said. "I'll give you that."

"For what?"

"For bringing upper trouble to our door on the same night another watcher goes missing."

Thena swore softly.

Cassian did not move.

The older figure stepped aside as someone else entered the room from the rear corridor, carrying a rain-dark coat and a strip of cloth marked with the same route cuts Cassian had followed from the ladder.

Not a greeting.

Evidence.

"So," the older figure said, eyes still on Cassian, "now we find out whether you're only being hunted."

"Or?" Thena asked.

The answer came clean.

"Or whether you're the reason one of ours did not come back."

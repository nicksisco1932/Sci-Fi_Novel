The outer door bowed inward.

The pressure latch held. The frame around it did not.

The biomechanic sent the attendants toward the inner passage. The older figure took the manual cutoff beside the west bulkhead. Cassian moved between the crèche bay and the door.

"You should be with Thena," the biomechanic said.

"I should be a lot of places."

"You can barely stand."

"Neither can Gor, if he gets in here."

The door buckled again.

The wall terminal woke, though its screen had been broken. A single amber light came on under the casing.

The older figure lifted the cutoff handle.

"Don't close the west bulkhead," Cassian said.

"It is the only way to keep him away from the patients."

"And keep the ward from escaping."

"There is nowhere to escape to."

The door gave a low metallic shriek.

The biomechanic looked at the pressure lines. "The return's already past the second mark. If we seal the bulkhead now, we cut the cooling branch."

The older figure lowered the handle by an inch.

"Then what?"

"We keep the branch open and move the patients."

"Gor is at the door."

"I noticed."

The door tore free of one hinge.

The first body through was not Gor's.

Alyen rolled beneath the frame, came up with a compact blade in one hand, and fired a single shot at Cassian.

He dropped behind the console. The shot struck the crèche screen. The membrane inside shuddered, but held.

The biomechanic shouted for the others to move.

Alyen crossed the room before Cassian could come around the console. She was faster than he remembered, her motion stripped down to entry and finish. He turned away from the second strike and took it along the shoulder. The blade caught fabric and skin.

He drove his forearm into her wrist. The weapon fell. She struck him across the mouth and reached for the strip at her belt.

Cassian caught her arm.

"Korin sent you to kill me."

"Yes."

"He knows Gor is here."

"Yes."

"He sent you anyway."

"Yes."

She tried to wrench free. Cassian used the table edge to turn her shoulder and put her against the wall. His ribs burned. His hand went numb. He held on long enough to see the dark strip fall from her belt.

Then the doorframe tore loose.

Gor entered through the breach.

He did not look at Alyen. He went straight toward the sealed bay.

"Carrier," he said.

Cassian released Alyen and stepped into Gor's path.

"You know what's in there?"

"I know enough."

"You don't."

"Move."

Cassian did not.

Gor drove forward. Cassian turned with the first strike and caught the second on his forearm. The impact threw him sideways into the isolation rail. He kept his feet only because the rail was there.

The biomechanic pulled a manual lever. The clear screen dropped between Gor and the crèche.

Gor struck it once.

The screen bent inward.

"There is nothing left to contain," Cassian said. "It's in the lines."

"Then the carrier is the line."

Gor struck the screen again. The lower brace tore free.

Alyen had recovered the dark strip. She moved for Cassian while his attention was on Gor.

Cassian saw the movement at the edge of his vision. He turned, caught her wrist, and pressed it down against the table. The strip skittered under the old preservation alcove door.

"You have no idea what that is," he said.

"I know what it does."

"No, you don't."

She looked past him toward Gor.

"Neither did Korin."

The screen fractured.

The biomechanic pulled the manual lock. The west bulkhead opened behind Cassian, revealing a short passage with no lights. The service chamber beyond had once been used for cryogenic recovery. A narrow cradle remained beneath a bank of dead instruments, its surface pitted and dry.

"That's not active," the biomechanic said. "Don't take him in there."

Gor hit the screen a third time. The upper frame broke.

"It doesn't need to be active," Alyen said.

She twisted Cassian's arm down and drove her shoulder into his chest. His grip broke. She ran for the strip.

Cassian caught the back of her coat, but the fabric tore. She reached the preservation alcove first and lifted the key.

Gor came through the broken screen.

The room narrowed to the space between them.

Cassian moved on the old form. He turned Gor away from the bay, took the first strike against his shoulder, and shifted under the next. Gor's weight drove him backward into the passage.

The preservation cradle was behind him.

"Cassian," the biomechanic said. "Out of there."

He could have moved.

Gor would have reached the crèche.

Cassian stayed.

Gor caught his collar and drove him against the cradle. The old frame groaned beneath both their weight. Cassian felt the metal bite through his shirt. He struck Gor under the jaw, not to hurt him, only to turn his head.

Gor's hand closed around Cassian's throat.

Alyen went to the control end of the cradle. The dark strip fit into a narrow recess beneath the manual panel. Nothing happened.

She turned it over, found a worn edge, and pressed it into the recess again.

A line of pale light appeared along the cradle.

The biomechanic stepped toward her. "Stop. You don't know what it will do."

"I know he'll go down."

"That's not the same."

"It has to be."

Gor heard her. He let Cassian fall and crossed the passage in two steps.

Alyen pulled at the strip. It had locked into the recess.

Gor seized her by the throat and lifted her from the floor. Her heels struck the cradle's side. Cassian drove his shoulder into Gor's back. Gor turned with him and threw him against the wall.

The strip's pale line reached the end of the cradle.

Alyen's hand found the manual release beneath its rim.

She pulled.

The service harness uncoiled from the cradle and caught Gor across the chest and hips. The metal floor kicked under Cassian's boots.

Gor tried to turn.

The old clamps locked against his armor and held him to the frame.

A second pulse traveled from the unique key into the cryo mechanism buried in Gor's augmentation. His implants went dark in sequence: first the arms, then the spine brace, then the respiratory support beneath his ribs.

He dropped to one knee.

One of the clamps tore free.

Alyen fell beside him. Her head struck the cradle edge. She did not rise.

Cassian crawled to Gor.

The biomechanic was already at Alyen's side. They checked her neck, then looked up once and shook their head.

The older figure pulled the strip from the console. It was black now, split down its center.

Gor's chest rose once, unevenly. The machine beneath his ribs made no sound.

His hand moved toward Cassian's wrist and stopped halfway.

"Don't," Cassian said.

Gor's eyes stayed open.

The skin around his mouth had gone gray. His breath came in a shallow, broken pull.

"You kept the old form," Gor said.

"It kept me alive."

"Not for long."

Cassian pressed a hand against Gor's shoulder. He could feel the weight of him and little else.

"You chose this."

Gor's gaze shifted to the sealed bay. "I chose more time."

"You chose the Hive."

"I chose not to watch Almelah end while I still had strength to stand."

"It ended anyway."

"Yes."

No defense followed.

The pause held between breaths.

"The oath was supposed to mean something," Gor said.

"It did."

"Not enough."

Cassian looked at him. "No."

Gor's mouth moved at one corner, not quite a smile.

"You never did know when to yield."

"You never did know when to stop."

The next breath did not come cleanly.

The biomechanic kept one hand on Gor's shoulder, though there was nothing left to adjust. The older figure stood over Alyen, the broken key in their palm.

From the far side of the ward came a low, distant rumble.

It passed beneath the floor and through the walls.

Gor heard it.

"What is that?"

No one answered.

The rumble came again, deeper this time, followed by a long vibration that shook dust from the old pipes.

The Hive's lights flickered.

Gor's eyes remained on Cassian.

"What did you bring back?"

Cassian wanted to answer.

He did not know.

The machine beneath Gor's ribs gave one final, incomplete pulse.

Then stopped.

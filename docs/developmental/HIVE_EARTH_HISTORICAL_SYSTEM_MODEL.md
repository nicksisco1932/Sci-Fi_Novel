# Hive Earth Historical System Model

> **Status:** Early development / non-canon. This is a causal working model, not a chronology to be canonized by implication. It synthesizes existing ecology, communication, weapon, Voss-history, timeline, and record-loss material without adding dates, fixed geography, casualty totals, factions, final demographic trajectories, or technology. Where this model offers a connection between anchors, that connection remains a developmental hypothesis unless explicitly marked otherwise.

## How to read this model

**Canon standing** and **confidence** are independent labels. An established anchor can be narrow or incomplete; a plausible hypothesis can be highly useful without becoming canon.

| Canon standing | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Established anchor | Already stated in canon-facing material or the continuity registry. |
| Derived constraint | A necessary limit or implication of established anchors; not a new event. |
| Developmental hypothesis | A coherent causal proposal for author use; not established. |
| Open branch | A deliberately unresolved alternative requiring later selection or evidence. |

| Confidence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| High confidence | Directly supported by several existing materials. |
| Plausible | Fits the anchors and known system pressures. |
| Speculative | Useful possibility with weak or indirect support. |
| Requires modeling | Cannot be responsibly fixed without quantitative, material, or social modeling. |
| Requires lore decision | Depends on an authorial choice that present material does not make. |

### State-vector key

Every era begins and ends with the same compact vector:

`P` population band; `E` energy dependence; `I` infrastructure density; `Eco` ecological condition; `C` political centralization; `Com` communication integration; `W` portable weapon accessibility; `A` class asymmetry.

Bands are comparative, not counts. `P0` dispersed/declining, `P1` low and uneven, `P2` recovering/regionally concentrated, `P3` dense and urbanizing, `P4` planetary-dense. Every population entry is **requires modeling**. Other terms are qualitative: low, mixed, high, or extreme. “Weapon accessibility” concerns portable projectile access, not knowledge of ballistics, strategic weapons, or every tool capable of harm.

### Invariants carried through every branch

- **Established anchor / high confidence:** Voss rises during the Great Sorrow and is later assassinated. The Great Union fractures after her death. The Voss Conquest occurs later, during that post-assassination fracture; it is not the cause of Voss’s rise or assassination.
- **Established anchor / high confidence:** Hive Earth is presently a mature Type I civilization. Type II remains future-facing; mature planetary gate rings do not imply a completed stellar enclosure.
- **Derived constraint / high confidence:** Harmony and Cognitive Architecture solve real coordination problems before they become instruments of stratification or control. Neither grants unrestricted mind reading or omnipotent command of individual behavior.
- **Derived constraint / high confidence:** Ecology is uneven recovery, not global death. Water, heat, food, coastlines, soil, and biological recovery remain materially unequal.
- **Derived constraint / high confidence:** Current projectile scarcity inside the Hive is an architectural and class outcome. Knowledge persists in historical, strategic, off-grid, and scavenger contexts.
- **Developmental hypothesis / speculative:** “Living substrate” is only shorthand for a possible emergent, materially dependent planetary cognitive ecology. It is not a canon system name, proof of consciousness, or explanation for all conflict.
- **Established anchor / high confidence:** Cassian’s encounter with quantum beings remains unresolved. It gives him no unique system control and does not establish a shared mechanism with Hive communication.

### Cassian’s historical bracket

**Established anchor / high confidence:** Cassian departs using legacy mission infrastructure and returns to a mature Hive Earth. **Open branch / requires lore decision:** this model assigns no numbered era to the departure and fixes no date. It belongs only to the broad legacy-to-Hive bracket: after the surface-age capacities that make the mission stack possible and before the mature system he encounters on return. Its exact relation to Reconstruction, early Harmony adoption, and the Voss chain remains intentionally open.

## Era records

### 1. Late Surface Age / Great Sorrow

- **Canon standing:** Established anchor, with developmental causal interpretation.
- **Confidence:** High confidence for crisis pressure and Voss’s rise; requires modeling for scale, population, and environmental timing.
- **Entry state:** `P2; E mixed fossil/industrial transition; I uneven legacy networks; Eco stressed and destabilizing; C fragmented; Com uneven networked; W broadly accessible; A high.`
- **Exit state:** `P1–P2; E emergency extraction plus improvised transition; I damaged and selectively protected; Eco acute uneven degradation; C consolidating around emergency authority; Com contested and unreliable; W high for organized actors; A extreme.`

**Initiating pressures and population scenario.** Heat, infrastructure failure, debt, weak schools, disaster pressure, and institutional breakdown define the Great Sorrow as a broad crisis label. Population does not need a single global trajectory: one branch holds dense urban regions through managed rationing while other regions lose service, move, or become statistically invisible. The band therefore remains `P2` entering and `P1–P2` leaving, **requires modeling**.

**Energy and environment.** Emergency relief, extraction, reconstruction, and military security compete for energy, water, transport, and labor. The Voss Effect’s later weather meaning preserves acid rain and extreme heat as cultural residue, not a complete climate diagnosis. Ecological harm is uneven: some systems are still productive or repaired while soil, water, coasts, and atmospheric exposure worsen elsewhere.

**Infrastructure, politics, class, communication, and weapons.** The surviving network favors nodes that can pay, command, or be made strategically useful. Voss’s administrative ascent can be modeled as turning fractured procurement, relief, extraction, and security into an interoperable power base. Communication is abundant enough to document and manipulate but not trusted enough to coordinate equitably. Projectile weapons remain ordinary instruments of armies, private security, and paramilitary structures; their accessibility follows industrial and political privilege rather than a future Hive rule.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** The bottleneck is not a lack of technical possibility but a failure to align maintenance, legitimacy, logistics, and attention. Relief can arrive as patronage, monitoring can become a substitute for repair, and emergency authority can preserve some systems by transferring risk downward.

**Winners, losers, and obscured populations.** Administrative coordinators, protected industries, security intermediaries, and locations on strategic supply routes gain leverage. Displaced households, precarious workers, disabled people whose care depends on continuity, and communities whose losses enter records only as contract failures are most obscured. Official archives preserve transfers and authorizations more reliably than the texture of ordinary survival.

**Inherited infrastructure and cultural habits.** Later eras inherit patched grids, extraction corridors, emergency registries, security contracting, compliance vocabulary, and the learned expectation that crisis makes exceptional control reasonable. The durable cultural habit is not obedience alone; it is the belief that survival requires someone to make a system move.

**Record bias and historical visibility.** Coalition and administrative materials make Voss-era coordination unusually visible while reducing ordinary life to inputs, losses, or risk categories. Material traces—acid loading, altered waterways, failed structures, reused equipment—can contradict clean institutional histories. Oral survival is strongest where people retain warning phrases, local repair knowledge, and memories that do not fit a ledger.

| Historical visibility | Working assessment |
|---|---|
| Elite record | High: offices, contracts, public claims, command accounts. |
| Institutional record | High but compromised: it preserves procedure and obscures motive. |
| Material record | Mixed: pollution, repair layers, and altered land persist unevenly. |
| Oral or tacit survival | Mixed: survival methods and phrases outlast official categories. |
| Ordinary-life visibility | Low: ordinary futures appear mainly as disrupted services or missing names. |

**Transition test: Great Sorrow -> Turning Time / Voss Consolidation.**

| Test | Working answer |
|---|---|
| Inherited pressure | Crisis systems cannot coordinate repair, legitimacy, and coercion at the same scale. |
| Enabling conditions | Existing administrative channels, extraction wealth, security capacity, and public exhaustion. |
| Causal mechanism | A coordinator makes emergency systems interoperable by binding relief, procurement, and command. |
| Reinforcement | Every successful delivery validates further central exception-making. |
| Counterpressure | Corruption, regional resistance, and the fact that relief is visibly uneven. |
| Credible alternative | Federated repair compacts, credible local authority, and transparent aid could reduce demand for a single consolidator. |
| Non-occurrence condition | The transition does not occur if emergency logistics remain accountable to independent local institutions that can deliver essential repair without routing access through one consolidating network. |

### 2. Turning Time / Voss Consolidation

- **Canon standing:** Established anchor for Voss, the Turning Time, Great Union association, and the Voss Effect; developmental hypothesis for system dynamics.
- **Confidence:** High confidence for sequence and contradiction; plausible for institutional mechanism.
- **Entry state:** `P1–P2; E emergency and extraction-dependent; I selective repair corridors; Eco acute uneven damage; C consolidating; Com contested; W high for state/private formations; A extreme.`
- **Exit state:** `P1–P2; E centrally allocated but brittle; I denser along priority routes; Eco managed in enclaves and worsening elsewhere; C high through the Great Union; Com standardized but distrustful; W concentrated among armed institutions; A extreme.`

**Initiating pressures and population scenario.** The consolidated system responds to real failures of allocation, warning, and command. Population remains a band rather than a count: consolidation can retain people in protected corridors while treating mobility, disappearance, and uncounted labor as administrative noise. This is **requires modeling**.

**Energy and environment.** Voss-era control makes energy and reconstruction capacity legible enough to allocate at scale. It does not resolve ecological damage. Programs can work and still transfer heat, waste, water loss, and exposure to places with less standing. The system’s advance toward Type I capacity is an established historical claim; the pathway, pace, and degree remain open.

**Infrastructure, politics, class, communication, and weapons.** The Great Union is a political formation, not proof of total unity. Standardized registries, contracts, security coordination, and priority infrastructure make authority portable across distance. Communication expands as an administrative necessity: a system that promises continuity needs trusted identity, visible queues, and a way to make orders travel. Its practical result can be selective legibility. Weapons concentrate in formal command and aligned paramilitary channels, while ordinary possession may remain widespread in unstable places.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** A system built on exceptions must continuously decide whose exception counts. Its corruption is not incidental: opaque legal channels can finance coordination while making the ledger itself an instrument of exclusion. The more smoothly it moves resources, the more catastrophic a legitimacy breach becomes.

**Winners, losers, and obscured populations.** Ascending administrators, compliant contractors, military command, protected cities, and populations whose labor can be measured become system-visible winners. People whose language, legal category, geography, or informal mutual aid cannot be standardized become residual. Their labor may be used while their political claims are archived as disorder.

**Inherited infrastructure and cultural habits.** Later systems receive a template: identity tied to service, emergency authority tied to infrastructure, corruption concealed by legality, and efficiency measured as continuity for those inside the queue. Under-Hive memory inherits the counter-template: mural, phrase, family warning, and the refusal to let administrative categories define the whole event.

**Record bias and historical visibility.** Elite and institutional records are especially dense here because consolidation produces paperwork. Material evidence shows which systems were actually built, maintained, or abandoned. Ordinary experience remains hard to retrieve because a successfully standardized identity can still conceal hunger, coercion, consent, and fear.

| Historical visibility | Working assessment |
|---|---|
| Elite record | Very high: leadership and administrative actors narrate their necessity. |
| Institutional record | Very high: registries, concessions, relief, security, and legal residue. |
| Material record | Mixed: built corridors persist; displaced lives do not leave equal traces. |
| Oral or tacit survival | High among affected communities, but fragmentary and politically dismissed. |
| Ordinary-life visibility | Low to mixed: present as data, absent as self-description. |

**Transition test: Turning Time / Voss Consolidation -> Post-Assassination Fracture / Voss Conquest.**

| Test | Working answer |
|---|---|
| Inherited pressure | Centralized continuity depends on a contested legitimacy and tightly coupled command. |
| Enabling conditions | Armed institutions, standardized enemy categories, emergency courts, and leaders able to claim Voss’s inheritance. |
| Causal mechanism | Voss’s assassination removes the person who could still contest claims made in her name; jurisdiction fragments into armed authority. |
| Reinforcement | Each local exception becomes a new government, then a patronage and security system with reasons to escalate. |
| Counterpressure | Shared infrastructure, exhausted populations, and officials invested in continuity rather than partition. |
| Credible alternative | A succession process with distributed command, protected minority status, and enforceable resource-sharing could preserve a reduced Union. |
| Non-occurrence condition | The later Conquest does not occur if post-assassination emergency councils retain cross-jurisdictional authority over food, water, and civilian protection and cannot redefine a minority population as outside continuity. |

### 3. Post-Assassination Fracture / Voss Conquest

- **Canon standing:** Established anchor for assassination, Great Union fracture, and later Voss Conquest; developmental hypothesis for the causal texture of aftermath.
- **Confidence:** High confidence for ordering; plausible for civil-system mechanics; requires modeling for duration and demographic effect.
- **Entry state:** `P1–P2; E centrally allocated but brittle; I dense in priority corridors and fragile elsewhere; Eco uneven damage; C fracturing from high control; Com standardized but contested; W concentrated and proliferating through conflict; A extreme.`
- **Exit state:** `P0–P1; E militarized and locally improvised; I damaged, captured, or repurposed; Eco scarred and unevenly recoverable; C fragmented; Com partitioned and distrusted; W high in war zones; A extreme.`

**Initiating pressures and population scenario.** The trigger is Voss’s assassination after her rise and consolidation; the Great Union fractures before the later Voss Conquest. The population band must not smuggle in a casualty total. It captures only a likely transition toward displacement, missing registries, and concentrated survival, **requires modeling**.

**Energy and environment.** War turns existing infrastructures into objectives. Grain, water, clinics, transport, and power routes become sources of force. The Conquest’s environmental trace need not be one global event: damaged catchments, interrupted harvests, polluted waterways, closed fisheries, and land rendered unsafe can become a patchwork archive.

**Infrastructure, politics, class, communication, and weapons.** The States of the Great Angel form from commands, offices, ministries, and emergency courts that discover a common enemy is stabilizing. Siege, census, and transport make administration part of violence. Communication remains available enough for records and propaganda while its authority is severed by faction. Portable projectiles and industrial weapons are accessible wherever command, supply, or scavenged stock can sustain them; violence is openly territorial rather than architecturally internalized.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** The essential failure is categorical: civilian survival becomes enemy capacity, then a population can be placed outside the future. Logistics that could preserve continuity become selection systems. The institution does not cease functioning; it functions toward clearance.

**Winners, losers, and obscured populations.** Surviving armed blocs, clerical authorities, transport systems, and officials who can convert uncertainty into paperwork gain authority. The Minority World-State and populations adjacent to it bear the disaster, but its precise boundaries remain deliberately unresolved. Children, households, informal care networks, local food keepers, and the displaced become least visible precisely when their survival becomes administratively decisive.

**Inherited infrastructure and cultural habits.** Later reconstruction inherits emptied districts, censuses, sealed routes, damaged water systems, displaced crafts, and administrative language that can render disappearance procedural. It also inherits mistrust of centralized records, manual survival protocols, hidden routes, and the long memory that a system promising preservation may define someone as disposable.

**Record bias and historical visibility.** Coalition records preserve stabilization, correction, and preservation; rebel accounts preserve extermination, names, and stories difficult to verify. Material evidence—harvest gaps, intake closures, disposal orders, quarantine, altered settlement patterns—can establish scale without delivering a total. Under-Hive murals join what institutional files keep separate.

| Historical visibility | Working assessment |
|---|---|
| Elite record | High but self-exculpating: orders and justifications survive. |
| Institutional record | High in fragments: censuses, transport, security, and closure records. |
| Material record | High for interruption and damage, low for individual meaning. |
| Oral or tacit survival | High in affected lineages; vulnerable to loss and dismissal. |
| Ordinary-life visibility | Very low: ordinary survival is the object acted upon, not the account preserved. |

**Transition test: Post-Assassination Fracture / Voss Conquest -> Reconstruction / Early Hive Construction.**

| Test | Working answer |
|---|---|
| Inherited pressure | Fragmented rule cannot reliably sustain water, food, repair, or civilian legitimacy. |
| Enabling conditions | Surviving technical corridors, displaced expertise, exhausted populations, and a need to make basic systems trustworthy again. |
| Causal mechanism | Reconstruction organizations couple life-support infrastructure to wider coordination standards and make service continuity the basis of renewed authority. |
| Reinforcement | Each functioning pump, clinic, or transit route increases dependence on interoperable maintenance. |
| Counterpressure | Trauma, anti-central memory, local sovereignty, and unequal access to rebuilt systems. |
| Credible alternative | Durable regional mutual-aid federations could rebuild interoperably without converging on a planet-spanning architecture. |
| Non-occurrence condition | Early Hive construction does not occur if the essential repair systems can remain locally governable and mutually compatible without a centralized identity, routing, and maintenance layer. |

### 4. Reconstruction / Early Hive Construction

- **Canon standing:** Derived constraint for later Hive continuity; developmental hypothesis for the reconstruction pathway.
- **Confidence:** Plausible; requires modeling for scale, energy mix, and population.
- **Entry state:** `P0–P1; E militarized/local improvisation; I damaged and repurposed; Eco scarred mosaic; C fragmented; Com partitioned; W high where conflict persists; A extreme.`
- **Exit state:** `P1–P2; E coordinated recovery systems; I growing nodes, conduits, and repair layers; Eco managed recovery mosaics; C layered coordination; Com interoperable civic services; W beginning to concentrate; A high.`

**Initiating pressures and population scenario.** Reconstruction begins because no household can individually restore interdependent systems. The model permits recovery through dense safe nodes, moving labor, and unevenly populated service zones rather than a single smooth rebound. All bands remain **requires modeling**.

**Energy and environment.** The goal is not a return to a vanished baseline. Early construction must stabilize water, heat, food, waste, and damaged coasts while energy systems expand. The durable ecological bargain is infrastructural: controlled food and water reduce some risks while making maintenance interruption more consequential.

**Infrastructure, politics, class, communication, and weapons.** “Early Hive” means a growing physical and administrative mesh—repair routes, climate controls, service registries, local compute, transit, and standard interfaces—not a completed city wrapping the planet. Political legitimacy shifts toward whoever can keep systems on. Communication integration begins as authentication, emergency warning, translation, route coordination, and care continuity. Weapons may remain common at edges and in legacy stock, but inhabited rebuilt zones start to punish uncontrolled discharge because shared walls and systems are becoming dense.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Materials, skilled maintenance, trusted records, contamination, power reliability, and the social legitimacy of registration are all limiting. A reconstruction state can confuse its need to know system load with a right to know every person. Peripheral areas can be attached enough to supply the core without receiving equal repair.

**Winners, losers, and obscured populations.** Engineers, water and food custodians, transit workers, archive owners, and communities situated on viable routes become visible. Displaced people, informal repairers, contaminated neighborhoods, and those whose identities cannot be reconciled with new registries risk being counted only as pressure on the system.

**Inherited infrastructure and cultural habits.** The era leaves vertical service density, standardized parts, life-support corridors, pooled repair knowledge, and a habit of accepting access credentials as a survival tool. It also leaves off-grid practices: manual route marks, local inspections, material repair, and suspicion of systems that promise help only through legibility.

**Record bias and historical visibility.** Institutional records improve because reconstruction depends on inventory and maintenance. They may misdescribe who kept a district alive. Material evidence retains repair seams, reused structures, unplanned settlements, and contaminated layers. Ordinary life survives more often as tacit craft than as a complete account.

| Historical visibility | Working assessment |
|---|---|
| Elite record | Mixed: planners and sponsors are overrepresented. |
| Institutional record | Growing: maintenance and registry evidence becomes dense. |
| Material record | High: repair layers, conduits, caps, and reused foundations. |
| Oral or tacit survival | High among crafts and local care networks. |
| Ordinary-life visibility | Low to mixed: visible as service demand, less as authorship. |

**Transition test: Reconstruction / Early Hive Construction -> Hive Consolidation / Harmony Adoption.**

| Test | Working answer |
|---|---|
| Inherited pressure | Interdependent repair has outgrown local coordination and incompatible standards. |
| Enabling conditions | Expanding interface capacity, civic identity, reliable local compute, and public memory of missed warnings and failed care. |
| Causal mechanism | A shared communication and governance architecture makes people, services, and decisions mutually addressable. |
| Reinforcement | Better evacuation, translation, distribution, and maintenance make voluntary adoption feel rational. |
| Counterpressure | Privacy, incompatible local practice, infrastructure cost, and memories of centralized violence. |
| Credible alternative | Strong interoperability standards with separable regional governance and meaningful offline rights. |
| Non-occurrence condition | Harmony is not adopted at scale if repair coordination can deliver equal reliability while identity, communication, and civic participation remain technically separable. |

### 5. Hive Consolidation / Harmony Adoption

- **Canon standing:** Established anchor for Harmony’s promise and Cognitive Architecture; developmental hypothesis for adoption history.
- **Confidence:** High confidence that it solved real problems and later stratified access; plausible for the adoption pathway.
- **Entry state:** `P1–P2; E coordinated recovery systems; I growing repair mesh; Eco managed mosaic; C layered; Com interoperable civic services; W concentrating; A high.`
- **Exit state:** `P2–P3; E planetary coordination dependent; I dense in core networks; Eco uneven but actively managed; C high through standards and routing; Com integrated by default; W low in intact civilian interiors; A high and encoded in service quality.`

**Initiating pressures and population scenario.** The adoption case must be strong: climate, transit, food, health, defense, and translation pressures make direct, authenticated coordination desirable. Population grows or re-concentrates only in broad bands, **requires modeling**; adoption does not prove equal inclusion.

**Energy and environment.** A more integrated system can manage planetary flows more competently, moving toward the Type I capability later visible in the mature Hive. It does not make climate, coastlines, soils, or nonhuman life uniformly healed. It makes the difference between well-served and low-priority ecology easier to route and hide.

**Infrastructure, politics, class, communication, and weapons.** Harmony’s core promise—shared cognition, layered intention, optimized governance—can develop from linked identity, translation, consent layers, semantic models, and local-to-planetary routing. This does not require unconstrained mind reading: intended communication remains a bounded act, and every link still depends on power, hardware, maintenance, and permissions. Cognitive Architecture becomes influential because access, priority, prediction, and error definitions shape conduct. As interiors become denser, a projectile discharge threatens services and bystanders; force shifts toward doors, traction, air, routing, machines, capture teams, and trained bodies.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Durable interface maintenance, consent, access equality, network error, and institutional temptation are primary bottlenecks. A consent membrane can exist while employment, school, care, transit, and citizenship make refusal materially costly. Central coordination can optimize for averages and still abandon a place whose loss looks acceptable in the model.

**Winners, losers, and obscured populations.** People with new, supported interfaces; stable services; trusted identities; and routes inside the standard gain agency and protection. Those with old hardware, bad translation, weak access, or no recognized path become governable without becoming well served. Their fatigue, repair labor, and local knowledge can vanish into a clean planetary dashboard.

**Inherited infrastructure and cultural habits.** Later Hive Earth inherits the expectation that contact, translation, and civic presence are ambient; that a technical system can mediate trust; and that availability is a moral virtue. It also inherits the first meaningful blind zones: places where physical proximity does not restore reachability because directory, routing, maintenance, or permission has failed.

**Record bias and historical visibility.** Harmony-era records can be abundant and still reductive. The authoritative rendering of a message, intent, or event may displace the uncertain human exchange. Material evidence continues to show power, heat, upkeep, and exclusion; oral survival protects dialects and sensorily local knowledge that standard models flatten.

| Historical visibility | Working assessment |
|---|---|
| Elite record | High: designers, executives, and policy rationales are retained. |
| Institutional record | Extreme: identity, routing, and service systems generate constant data. |
| Material record | High but hidden in infrastructure layers and maintenance debt. |
| Oral or tacit survival | Mixed: local terms persist where standard models misread them. |
| Ordinary-life visibility | Mixed to low: behavior is visible; consent and felt experience are not. |

**Transition test: Hive Consolidation / Harmony Adoption -> Mature Hive Earth / Type I Integration.**

| Test | Working answer |
|---|---|
| Inherited pressure | Rising density and planetary environmental load demand coordinated energy, transit, climate, and maintenance decisions. |
| Enabling conditions | Integrated identity, high-density infrastructure, learned interfaces, standard protocols, and expanding energy systems. |
| Causal mechanism | The same coordination stack is extended from essential services to planetary-scale flows and megastructure operations. |
| Reinforcement | Reliability makes deeper integration normal; benefits become hard to separate from the network. |
| Counterpressure | Waste heat, maintenance burden, privacy conflict, periphery neglect, and systemic single points of failure. |
| Credible alternative | A polycentric Type I arrangement with interoperable but separately governed energy and communication systems. |
| Non-occurrence condition | Mature Type I integration does not occur if energy, transit, and climate systems cannot be coordinated without creating maintenance and legitimacy burdens greater than the service gains. |

### 6. Mature Hive Earth / Type I Integration

- **Canon standing:** Established anchor for present Type I capability, gate-ring network, Harmony legacy, and unequal Hive condition; developmental hypothesis for system-wide causal relations.
- **Confidence:** High confidence for capability and unevenness; plausible for some operating mechanisms; requires modeling for population.
- **Entry state:** `P2–P3; E planetary coordination dependent; I dense core networks; Eco actively managed mosaic; C high by standards/routing; Com integrated by default; W low in intact interiors; A high.`
- **Exit state:** `P3–P4; E extreme planetary dependence; I extreme but aging unevenly; Eco restoration plus accumulated stress; C high with widening local exceptions; Com near-ambient but quality-stratified; W low in core/high in margins; A extreme.`

**Initiating pressures and population scenario.** The mature system persists because it makes genuine large-scale life possible. Present-era scenario planning must retain the existing **9-, 12-, and 18-billion** possibilities only as alternatives, all **requires modeling**. This era uses `P3–P4`, not a settled number or trajectory.

**Energy and environment.** Type I is current capacity: the civilization can command planetary-scale energy and coordinate large environmental systems. Its power has physical routes and receiving ends—generation, cooling, water, waste, orbital exchange, and maintenance. Type II remains a future direction. Ecology is both competence and debt: restored systems can exist beside altered oceans, damaged soils, buried rivers, constrained coasts, and districts carrying exported heat or waste.

**Infrastructure, politics, class, communication, and weapons.** Multiple moon-class gate structures and mature Hive infrastructure demonstrate scale without authorizing a perfect planet. Centrality appears through standards, executive clearance, prioritized routes, and the ability to define anomaly. Communication can feel intimate and instantaneous on Earth while still being dependent on edge routing, energy, hardware, consent, and local semantic models. The high-status form of violence is environmental and administrative: deny access, alter traction or air, isolate a route, classify a person, redirect care, or deploy a trained body. Projectile weapons are scarce among ordinary core civilians because a shot interacts with a dense, sensing building; they remain meaningful in off-grid and scavenger networks, as guarded community assets, in history, and beyond the inhabited-Hive rule.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** The mature failure is not necessarily collapse. It is maintenance prioritization: an intact core can coexist with services that flicker, route around, or cease to recognize the periphery. Systemic danger grows when a civilization treats model confidence as lived reality, or when future expansion is assumed to solve debts that present maintenance cannot.

**Winners, losers, and obscured populations.** Core residents, Arkline-like elites, executive institutions, supported-interface users, and people protected by priority service gain a world that anticipates need. Lower Grid residents, blind-zone communities, obsolete-interface users, and workers who keep material systems alive outside recognized visibility carry the cost. The official record sees their usage and irregularity more readily than their knowledge, consent, or care.

**Inherited infrastructure and cultural habits.** The era leaves planetary rings, spindles, service layers, personal models, trusted silence for elites, and the civic assumption that being reachable is normal. It also preserves counter-habits: manual checks, hand-marked routes, community-held weapons, repair apprenticeship, guarded offline conversation, and distrust of an answer delivered by a system that cannot explain its inputs.

**Record bias and historical visibility.** This is the paradoxical record-rich era. Elite and institutional visibility are extreme; data can survive as unreadable, permission-locked, normalized, or misclassified residue. Material record is vast but hidden behind later construction. Tacit survival holds practical truths about runoff, old hardware, dialect, food, and repair that planetary cognition may not collect. Ordinary life can be exhaustively tracked and still poorly known.

| Historical visibility | Working assessment |
|---|---|
| Elite record | Extreme: clearance, policy, and private archives may be preserved locally. |
| Institutional record | Extreme but curated: data volume is not human explanation. |
| Material record | Extreme but layered, repurposed, and difficult to interpret. |
| Oral or tacit survival | High where communities remain outside standard routing. |
| Ordinary-life visibility | Paradoxically low in meaning despite high behavioral capture. |

**Transition test: Mature Hive Earth / Type I Integration -> Maintenance Fracture / Uneven Continuity.**

| Test | Working answer |
|---|---|
| Inherited pressure | Extreme density and system complexity make universal maintenance more costly than expansion or core preservation. |
| Enabling conditions | Priority algorithms, service tiering, obsolete hardware, hidden infrastructure debt, and populations with weak political visibility. |
| Causal mechanism | Maintenance is deferred or rerouted unevenly; degradation becomes a managed background condition rather than a declared crisis. |
| Reinforcement | Stable core service conceals peripheral evidence and validates further prioritization. |
| Counterpressure | Shared-system risk, local expertise, off-grid survival, and the moral or practical cost of blind zones. |
| Credible alternative | Transparent maintenance obligations, distributive repair authority, and genuine offline rights could keep unevenness from hardening. |
| Non-occurrence condition | The fracture does not occur if every service tier has enforceable repair guarantees and local evidence can override central models before degradation becomes structural. |

### 7. Maintenance Fracture / Uneven Continuity

- **Canon standing:** Derived constraint from present core/periphery contrast and blind zones; developmental hypothesis as an era label.
- **Confidence:** High confidence for uneven continuity; plausible for the maintenance-fracture mechanism.
- **Entry state:** `P3–P4; E extreme planetary dependence; I extreme but aging unevenly; Eco restoration plus accumulated stress; C high with exceptions; Com near-ambient but stratified; W low in core/high in margins; A extreme.`
- **Exit state:** `P3–P4; E still planetary but priority-bound; I seamless in favored zones and degraded below; Eco curated recovery plus unmanaged pockets; C formally high and practically uneven; Com strong in core/partial in blind zones; W class-located; A extreme and spatially visible.`

**Initiating pressures and population scenario.** No demographic claim is needed beyond continued high density under the present 9/12/18-billion scenario set, **requires modeling**. “Fracture” means a discontinuity in care, recognition, and maintenance rather than a simple fall of the whole civilization.

**Energy and environment.** Cooling, water, food, waste, and air remain functional systems, but the costs travel downward and outward. A core wetland may be genuinely restored while an unregistered community loses pumping rights; a lower district can identify a treatment failure by smell before a model recognizes it. Uneven ecology is an operating feature of class, not scenery.

**Infrastructure, politics, class, communication, and weapons.** The core keeps its uplinks, services, predictive systems, and compliance culture. The Lower Grid, lanes between signals, Below the Map, and other blind-zone conditions emerge from abandoned layers, degraded hardware, partial routing, and human adaptation—not from a single outside faction. Formal political centralization remains high, but practical authority is shared with local survival networks. Projectile craft persists through workshops, caches, and apprenticeship; a community may hold a weapon as a collective liability rather than a casual sidearm. Blades remain common because they are repairable tools and controllable close-range force.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** The key bottleneck is whether the system can recognize a problem before people must solve it locally. Failure modes include weak sensors, bad model fit, degraded translation, unsupported implants, physical corrosion, denied repair, and the conversion of a local warning into an unprioritized anomaly.

**Winners, losers, and obscured populations.** The winners are not necessarily malicious individuals: they are those whose service quality is treated as baseline. Losers are people whose lives arrive as outage reports, noncompliance flags, medical backlog, or unauthorized occupancy. The most obscured population consists of those whose manual expertise keeps zones habitable while their identities remain absent or suspect.

**Inherited infrastructure and cultural habits.** The present inherits the full Hive and its shadow: dead terminals capable of brief use, maintenance shafts, relay gaps, old pumpworks, human signals, local watch systems, and a culture that asks what a building will do before trusting what a person says. It also inherits elite disconnection as privilege: the powerful can reach others while controlling their own reachability.

**Record bias and historical visibility.** Core records can classify a lower zone as deprioritized, yet residents experience crowding, failed care, and brittle survival. Material and tacit evidence become relatively more trustworthy locally: rust color, water smell, tool marks, route practice, family knowledge, and which system has stopped being safe.

### 8. Present Novel Era

- **Canon standing:** Established anchor for the unified Hive-run Earth, core/periphery contrast, blind zones, Cassian’s return, and constrained Reversionist intervention; developmental hypothesis for historical synthesis.
- **Confidence:** High confidence for current conditions; speculative for any deeper system interpretation.
- **Entry state:** `P3–P4; E planetary and priority-bound; I seamless in favored zones/degraded below; Eco curated recovery plus unmanaged pockets; C formally high/practically uneven; Com strong in core/partial in blind zones; W class-located; A extreme.`
- **Exit state:** `Open: this model deliberately does not project a post-novel state.`

**Initiating pressures and population scenario.** Cassian’s return exposes an existing contradiction rather than causing the historical system. The present retains only broad 9-, 12-, and 18-billion alternatives, all **requires modeling**. Cassian’s own temporal displacement makes him a partial witness: he preserves context the future has flattened, but not the lost centuries that produced the present.

**Energy and environment.** Hive Earth’s Type I capacity is real and operational. Its ecological competence is also real, but distributed through protected spindles, degraded zones, buried water, altered coastlines, controlled food, and service priority. A completed Type II system remains absent. No environmental condition should be used as evidence that the entire planet is dead or healed.

**Infrastructure, politics, class, communication, and weapons.** In high-integration areas, Cognitive Architecture works through care, access, classification, surveillance, prediction, language, and physical architecture. It has cracks: damaged systems, local choice, ambiguity, and people outside reliable routing matter. Communication is not a shared omniscient mind. It is a layered, powered, permissioned network whose rich and poor versions differ sharply. The Reversionist’s help is local, partial, and bounded by damaged infrastructure. Projectile weapons remain socially located; close combat, environmental force, and controlled architecture dominate many current Hive encounters.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** The immediate bottleneck is not whether the Hive can act, but who can define an anomaly, whose evidence reaches a decision-maker, and what systems can be trusted below the threshold of full visibility. A network can carry a true message with false urgency, authenticate an identity while coercing its model, or preserve data while losing the ordinary meaning of an event.

**Winners, losers, and obscured populations.** Core residents and executive institutions inhabit anticipatory protection. Lower Grid and Below-the-Map communities live with manual checks, incomplete care, and local authority. Cassian, Thena, and other individuals should not become system keys: their importance is situated—in testimony, action, local relation, and choices—not unique control over the planet.

**Inherited infrastructure and cultural habits.** The present makes every prior era legible as residue: Voss phrases and murals, damaged and repurposed water systems, hierarchy built into service flows, persistent repair crafts, elite privacy, historic weapons knowledge, and the assumption that a system record is never the same thing as the whole life it records.

**Record bias and historical visibility.** The present can access immense archives while repeating their exclusions. Cassian’s experience and under-Hive memory are corrective viewpoints, not total alternatives. The quantum beings remain especially uncertain: collective contact may resemble a pattern in the Hive, but resemblance does not prove identity, intention, or causation.

| Historical visibility | Working assessment |
|---|---|
| Elite record | Extreme and clearance-bound. |
| Institutional record | Extreme, operationally useful, and structurally selective. |
| Material record | Everywhere: infrastructure, water, heat, repair, and decay. |
| Oral or tacit survival | Essential below formal visibility. |
| Ordinary-life visibility | Still incomplete; abundance of data does not repair absence of meaning. |

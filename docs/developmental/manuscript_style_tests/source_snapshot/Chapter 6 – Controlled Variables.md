[[Cassian Rho|Cassian]] sat alone in the observation chamber, staring at the pale surface of the table in front of him. No restraints. No visible sensors. Just a low hum from the walls and the faint scent of sterilized air. Nothing to push against except the room itself.

His thoughts refused to hold shape. He had tried—tried hard—to reconstruct the final moments before the jump collapsed. The wave. The anomaly. The AI’s voice: There is an anomaly in the destination field.

He remembered that clearly.

And then—

Nothing.

A void.

His mind searched for purchase, but the fragments refused to align. There were images—impressions—pressure behind his eyes and something darker threaded through the static. Every time he reached for it, it slipped deeper out of reach.

Why had [[Korin Tal|Korin]] asked about hallucinations?

Why the practiced charm? The evasive precision?

Cassian didn’t believe in kindness without motive.

Whatever had followed him back… Korin was probing for it.

And Cassian had nothing to offer—except instinct.

The door hissed open. Korin entered again, his posture as relaxed as ever. He carried nothing but a small data slate.

“Mr. Rho,” he said with a diplomatic nod. “We’re going to begin cognitive rehabilitation today. Nothing invasive. Just some preliminary tasks—reflexive pattern analysis, memory tethering, symbol reconstruction.”

Cassian raised an eyebrow. “That sounds specific.”

“It’s designed to stimulate dormant neural layers affected by long-term quantum displacement. The protocols are quite old—salvaged from deep-space reconditioning programs.”

Cassian didn’t argue. He needed more information. If cooperation bought him time, he would take it.

Korin placed the slate on the table. It projected a calm, almost meditative interface—geometric patterns, subtle movement, ambient light. Harmless on the surface.

“Just follow the instructions. No rush,” Korin said, turning to leave. “We’ll be observing cognitive resonance and response intervals. If anything feels… difficult, take your time.”

Cassian didn’t look up. “And if it feels easy?”

Korin paused in the doorway.

“Then we’ll calibrate something harder.”

The door closed.

Cassian stared at the interface.

Tests.

Or traps.

---

Time passed. He wasn’t sure how much. The sequences grew more complex—symbols rotating into impossible geometries, logic chains folding back on themselves, questions that weren’t really questions.

Cassian played along.

Until the door opened again.

This time it wasn’t Korin.

A woman entered as if the room had only been waiting for her.

Tall. Efficient. Eyes sharp enough to cut alloy. Her uniform bore no insignia. It didn’t need one.

She didn’t introduce herself.

She didn’t have to.

“Mr. Rho,” she said, voice as cold as vacuum. “You’re not being honest with us.”

Cassian leaned back, assessing. “I didn’t know I owed you the truth.”

“You owe [[Hive Spindle World Document|Hive Earth]] everything,” she replied, stepping closer. There was no anger in it. Only doctrine. “You’re alive because we permitted it.”

“So this is the nice part over?”

She didn’t smile. “Begin sequence 7. Override safeguards.”

Cassian’s terminal shifted. The ambient light vanished. New glyphs appeared—erratic, pulsing, too fast to track.

He blinked. Pain stabbed behind his eyes.

“Stop,” he said.

“Continue,” she said, unmoved. “You’ll tell us what was in that vessel, or we will keep building pressure until silence stops feeling possible.”

He clenched his jaw as the symbols stopped behaving like images and fractured into pulses. Not visual. Neural.

His hands locked around the edge of the table.

She moved closer. “My name is [[Alyen Ithra]]. I serve Director Tal directly. I will extract what’s inside you if I have to peel your cognition apart layer by layer.”

Cassian’s vision blurred. The voice. The warning. A phrase rising through static:

Stop the expansion.

“I don’t remember,” he ground out.

She leaned in. “Then we keep going until your body remembers for you.”

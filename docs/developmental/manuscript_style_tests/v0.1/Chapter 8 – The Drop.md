[[Cassian Rho|Cassian]] couldn’t breathe.

He couldn’t see. The panel had given way beneath him before he fully understood what he had escaped into, and now the shaft had become a larger tube, less passage than machine.

It was a wind tunnel designed for maintenance drones, not human bodies. Air hammered through it with engineered force, clawing at every inch of him and trying to pin him backward even as it dragged him deeper. He tucked tight, arms close, body streamlined, but it wasn’t enough; direction meant nothing in the violence of it, and he was accelerating the wrong way with no way to stop.

He caught flashes—cabling, fan blades, pressure valves. Nothing sized for a person. Nothing built to forgive an organic body.

Then—

A flicker ahead. An opening. A rectangular void in the left wall. Deliberately cut. Deliberately left open.

Cassian had seconds.

The airflow slammed him sideways, roaring in his ears.

He flared his body against it. The force ripped at his joints. The opening came too fast to judge cleanly.

He twisted. Kicked.

Slammed into the edge of the breach.

His ribs shrieked. His shoulder crunched. Something tore.

But he was out.

Whether that meant survival or only a different impact, he could not tell.

He tumbled into darkness, bounced once, then struck metal hard enough to flash his vision white.

And then—

Nothing.

---

Memory crashed back with the impact.

For a moment, he could not tell whether he was remembering or falling again.

Cassian stood in combat stance. Oxygen thin. Gravity light. Powdered ash shifted under his boots.

Across from him: an eight-foot-tall figure—lanky, coiled, bipedal. No face. No armor. Just pale gray skin drawn tight over long limbs, humanoid but not human. Not quite.

It moved.

Cassian reacted first.

He slipped under the opening swing, pivoted around a second, drove an elbow into its side. Low kick to the knee. Heel-turn. Hammerfist across where a neck should have been. Strikes meant to break structure, breath, intention.

The thing took them without a sound.

Cassian pressed. Knee. Palm-strike. Uppercut into the place where a human jaw would have folded. Still nothing. No stagger. No breath change. No pain response he could read.

He changed angles. Baited it forward. Closed hard. Three quick jabs, a feint left, a shoulder drive that slammed it into a stone pillar. He twisted behind it, locked an arm across its throat, and drove it down into the ash.

Dust burst.

The thing lay still.

Cassian backed off.

Waited.

It rose without hurry. No limp. No hesitation.

Then it changed.

Now it was not receiving.

It was answering.

Faster. Cleaner. As if the exchange had taught it something.

Cassian barely registered the shift before the flurry began. An open-palm strike snapped his head sideways. A reverse step crushed his ribs. A sweeping leg clipped his knee and dropped him just enough for a spinning elbow to hammer his shoulder.

He staggered.

It kept coming.

Cassian parried three blows, four, then the fifth clipped his temple. He turned into a counter—too late. It was reading him now, the counter waiting before he committed to it.

It was done testing.

Cassian reached for everything he had. Helion training. Boarding drills. Improvised violence. Grapples. Throws. Strikes to nerves and joints. Nothing landed hard enough. Nothing slowed it for long. Whatever did land only seemed to teach it more.

The creature’s final strike lifted him into the air.

And hurled him off the cliff.

He tore through branches on the way down. Rolled. Bounced. Came to in blood and dirt and sky.

Defeated. Alive by accident.

Then—

A drone. Humming low. Floating beside him. His ship’s insignia burned across its chassis.

> "Recovery sequence initiated. Stay conscious."

He dragged himself through alien ferns beneath violet trees. The planet throbbed with sound—distant hums, electrical pulses, something vast moving just beyond comprehension.

He reached an overlook and looked back toward the cliff.

They were still there.

Thousands of them. Lined along the ridgeline.

Silent.

Watching.

And in unison—

Their minds touched his.

> "Stop the expansion."

The words bypassed hearing.

They landed whole.

Etched themselves there.

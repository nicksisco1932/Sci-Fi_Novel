The rain thickened into a curtain, cold enough to sting and flatten the world into moving sheets of silver. [[Cassian Rho|Cassian]] stepped into it and let it strike bruised ribs and split skin. It had been centuries since he'd felt real weather. Not simulation. Not controlled atmospheric mist. Rain.

For a few seconds, he let it touch him.

Thena watched from under the broken overhang. "You good?"

He did not answer. The mineral smell rising off rusted grates, the hard percussion on metal, the low thunder rolling through the fractured dome - it was honest. Briefly, brutally honest.

Then he heard it.

A vibration under the rainfall.

At first it could have been deep-grid machinery. Then it resolved into rhythm. Weight. Intention.

Footfalls.

Thena stiffened. "Cassian-"

He raised a hand. "Quiet."

The sound came closer without haste. No attempt to hide it. Whoever was moving wanted the warning to arrive first.

Lightning cracked across the sector.

In that white flash, the shape hit him.

A shoulder drove into Cassian's ribs with enough force to lift him off his feet and throw him through the adjacent wall. Metal screamed around him. He landed hard on the other side in a rain of dust and broken plating.

Pain flooded his vision.

A silhouette stepped through the hole he had made.

Massive. Balanced. Entirely at ease.

"You're getting soft, Cassian."

The voice cut through the rain with practiced contempt. Familiar in shape if not yet in name.

Cassian tried to rise.

Too slow.

A blade spun past him and buried itself through Thena's hand into the wall. Her shout bounced off the concrete.

The man did not even turn to look at her.

He rolled his neck once, stepped fully into the room, and grabbed Cassian by the front of the shirt with one hand. Cassian's boots barely touched the floor.

Rain ran down the attacker's face as he grinned.

"Look at you. Taking time for weather."

A short laugh. "Soft."

He hurled Cassian across the room.

Cassian hit a support beam hard enough to bend it and dropped in a heap, breath torn from him. The attacker came after him at an easy pace, arms loose, enjoying the space between blows.

"What is this?" he shouted over the rain. "This is what the [[Hive Spindle World Document|Hive]]'s lost wanderer looks like now?"

He laughed again, louder this time.

Thena fought the knife with her free hand. Blood ran down her wrist and mixed with rainwater leaking through the ceiling.

Cassian forced himself upright against the beam. Every breath bit deep, but his eyes stayed on the man.

The grin widened.

"Better," the attacker said. "Get up. I didn't come this far to beat a ghost."

Cassian's mind ran the geometry and found nothing clean. No line through the man. No direct win.

He flicked a glance toward Thena.

She read it at once.

"Down," he said.

She dropped, tearing her hand free with a sound she swallowed before it became a scream.

Cassian snatched a broken length of metal from the floor and threw it into the weakened cabling overhead.

Sparks. A snap. Then a section of support tore loose and came crashing down.

The attacker shifted one step back, annoyed more than hurt.

One step was enough.

Cassian grabbed Thena and drove her toward a half-collapsed service hatch behind the debris. The attacker lunged. Cassian shoved Thena through and followed a beat before a massive hand slammed into the hatch from the other side.

Boom.

Dust sifted from the ceiling.

A second impact dented the metal inward.

Thena clutched her wounded hand. "What is he?"

Cassian pulled her deeper into the crawlspace. "Don't know."

Another blow hit the hatch. Then another. Slow. Deliberate. Almost patient.

"He's tracking us?" Thena asked.

"No," Cassian said. "He's enjoying the chase."

At the first dark junction Cassian paused only long enough to read slope, echo, and cover.

Left.

They ran.

Water splashed underfoot. Their breathing knocked off the walls. Far behind them, the man's voice threaded through the tunnels, calm and perfectly placed.

"Run, Cassian. Run while you still remember how."

That chilled him more than the rain.

Blood marked their path, bright on the dark floor. Cassian saw it and quickened their pace.

This was not Hive security. Not a scav ambush. Not anything simple.

This was personal.

Someone who knew him.

Someone who had waited for him to slow down.

He would not give that man the same opening twice.

They ran deeper into the tunnels, toward whatever shelter the off-grid lanes still offered.

The rain fell away behind them.

The hunt did not.

# Manuscript Style Guide v0.2 — Rhythm and Development

**Frozen before the v0.2 prose pass, after review of baseline-to-v0.1 changes.** This is a non-canon experiment guide. Chapter 16 is a reference for connected attention, not a statistical target.

## Central principle

**Let related perceptions develop through sentences rather than compressing them into inventories.**

## Practice

1. Develop a descriptive passage through spatial relationships, bodily constraints, and shifts of attention already supported by the scene.
2. Vary syntax and emphasis. Keep short beats where they mark interruption, danger, silence, or an actual turn.
3. Change diction only when it improves precision or restores authorial texture; avoid ornamental synonym swaps.
4. Preserve each viewpoint’s habits of attention. Sustained observation, immediate action, contested exchange, recollection/recovery, and signal/countdown are useful scene modes that may transition; none dictates a character’s cadence.
5. Preserve evidence before inference, uncertainty, dialogue meaning, and the timing of reveals. Keep narration, dialogue, and interface messages distinct.
6. Add no sensory facts, history, lore, motives, or emotional explanations. No sentence- or paragraph-length quotas.

## Review lens

Check connection, spatial clarity, bodily constraint, viewpoint, subtext, and pacing against both earlier versions and the surrounding scene. Record each revision, retained passage, and exception in the version ledger.

# Prose Style Guide v0.1 — Chapter 16 Trial

**Status: frozen for this trial.** This guide derives its stylistic observations solely from `Chapter 16 – Dead Relay.md`. It is a hypothesis for a separate manuscript experiment, not canon or a mandate to make every scene sound alike.

## What to carry forward

1. **Follow a connected line of attention.** Let a paragraph move through related perceptions, bodily responses, and practical thought. In Chapter 16, sound in the walls leads into Cassian's physical strain and his tactical scan; the paragraph develops one moment instead of presenting three unrelated beats.
2. **Let physical description acquire meaning through use.** The relay chamber's shapes and damage, the maintenance shaft's geometry and grooves, and the reservoir chamber's air, scale, and infrastructure all arise as the characters move through them.
3. **Give evidence before inference.** A detail may change what a character suspects or chooses. Keep the interpretation attached to what the character can observe, and preserve uncertainty where the scene has not resolved it.
4. **Let paragraph shape follow the scene's attention.** Group existing sentences when they concern the same moment, place, action, and viewpoint. Recast syntax or add a small transition only to make that existing relationship clear. Use no target paragraph length.
5. **Vary texture.** Connected paragraphs can carry sustained observation; short paragraphs, fragments, dialogue, and silence can keep their force at turns in attention, interruption, danger, or revelation.
6. **Let the world show its use and condition.** Wear, repair, working systems, and damaged infrastructure can suggest history through existing evidence, without pausing for an explanation.

## Chapter 16 anchors

- Opening tunnel passage: environmental sound, Cassian's strained body, and tactical attention form one perceptual movement.
- Relay chamber: architecture and accumulated damage make abandonment legible.
- Maintenance shaft: bodily recovery and the route's physical constraints share the same scene movement.
- Reservoir arrival: air and smell open into scale, catwalks, water, conduits, and the sector's condition.

These passages illustrate connected attention in different rhythms. They do not establish sentence counts, recurring metaphors, Cassian's tactical voice as a universal viewpoint, or the chapter's countdown and isolated messages as templates.

## Trial method

- Recast existing sentences and regroup paragraphs where it helps one observation develop continuously.
- Use only information already established in the passage or relevant source scene. Add no sensory fact, history, lore, action, motive, or emotional conclusion.
- Leave a passage alone when it already carries the intended depth and rhythm.
- Keep a short beat when it performs a specific job. Do not lengthen for appearance or make every scene follow the reference chapter's cadence.
- Record a chapter as revised, already suitable, or retained with an exception. Explain exceptions briefly without changing this frozen guide.

## Review questions

- Does the paragraph sustain one point-of-view character's attention through related details?
- Does description affect what the character can do, notice, or infer in that moment?
- Does the prose distinguish observed evidence from uncertain interpretation?
- Does the surrounding scene retain its spatial clarity, tension, silence, and distinct character voices?
- Did the revision add explanation or emphasis that the original left unresolved?

## Boundaries

- The *Children of Dune* image expresses the author's aspiration only. Do not borrow its wording, imagery, sentence patterns, or style.
- Chapter 16 is the sole stylistic source for v0.1. Other chapters are material to test, not sources for expanding the guide.
- Preserve canon, event order, character knowledge and motives, dialogue intent, point of view, ambiguity, terminology, scene breaks, emphasis, and wikilinks.
- This guide remains fixed through v0.1. Any guide changes belong in a later version such as v0.2.

The newcomer laid the rain-dark coat across the table with both hands, careful not to smear what was already on it.

Water.

Mineral grit.

A darker stain at one cuff that was not quite blood and not quite mud.

The strip of marked cloth went beside it.

Route cuts.

Handmade.

One edge torn hard enough to leave threads splayed like wire.

No one in the room sat except Cassian and Thena.

No one touched the evidence after it was placed.

Even here, below the map, procedure had a shape.

The older figure remained standing at the head of the table.

"South watch missed second report," they said. "This was found snagged on pipe mesh near the runoff cut."

Thena looked from the coat to the cloth and then to the older figure.

"And that becomes our fault how?"

"It becomes your problem because the timing overlaps," the older figure said.

The runner who had carried the items in stayed by the rear door, rain still clinging to the shoulders of their jacket. They did not speak. They had already delivered what mattered.

Cassian looked at the coat.

Not at the story around it.

At the object.

The wet had dried unevenly. One sleeve carried a clean line where pressure had squeezed runoff through fabric. The collar seam was stretched on one side, not torn open but stressed hard enough to whiten the thread.

He said, "How long missing?"

The older figure's eyes narrowed a fraction.

"Long enough to matter."

"That isn't a time."

"An hour and eleven minutes since last check signal."

Thena turned to Cassian.

"You hearing this? We crossed the line and got searched inside that window. Unless you think I had time to murder someone between being accused and being searched."

"I think nothing yet," the older figure said. "Which is why you're still sitting down."

Cassian kept looking at the coat.

"Who found it?"

"Boundary watch on the next shift."

"What else?"

"A dropped lantern. Drag marks to the runoff lip. No body."

Thena leaned back in her chair. "That sounds less like us and more like a problem you already had."

The older figure ignored her.

"You said something followed you forward," they said to Cassian. "Start again. Cleaner."

Cassian's eyes left the coat at last.

"I was on approach to a mission jump. The onboard intelligence flagged an anomaly in the destination field. Then I woke here. Four centuries late."

The masked watcher by the wall shifted once.

"That's your cleaner version?"

"Yes," Cassian said.

"And the dead systems?" the older figure asked. "The terminals. The route. The floodgates."

"Not the same thing," Cassian said.

"Explain the difference."

"One warned me before the jump. One is helping me now."

That held the room for a beat.

Thena rubbed her thumb once against the edge of the table.

"Told you the answers get worse when you press him."

The older figure did not look away from Cassian.

"You expect me to believe there are two separate ghosts moving around your life."

"I expect you to believe I don't confuse a warning with an extraction route."

"That's a convenient distinction."

"It's also true."

The runner at the rear door gave two light taps against the frame.

Not a knock.

Request to speak.

The older figure tilted their head once in permission.

"Found this too," the runner said, and placed a snapped blade beside the coat.

Ceramic.

Homemade handle wrap.

Broken near the grip.

Cassian reached for it automatically.

The masked watcher moved at once, weapon lifting.

"Ask first," they said.

Cassian looked at the older figure.

"May I?"

The older figure let the pause run long enough to make the point clear.

Then nodded.

Cassian picked up the blade.

The break was blunt, not chipped from striking metal. The remaining edge carried fabric threads and a faint smear of skin oil. No blood.

He set it down.

"Your watcher got one strike at close range," he said.

No one answered.

He touched the stretched collar seam of the coat.

"Then got taken high and hard from this side."

The older figure said, "Taken."

"Not killed where the coat was found."

"How can you tell?"

"No collapse in the shoulder. No blood sink. No panic tearing at the front closure." He nudged the broken blade with one finger. "And this snapped in a grip fight, not on impact with a wall."

Thena studied the coat now with new attention.

"One person?"

"Probably."

The masked watcher sounded unconvinced.

"You're giving a lot of certainty to wet fabric."

"I'm giving certainty to force application," Cassian said. "A Hive snatch team leaves different evidence. Restraint tags. Suppressor residue. Controlled mess. This is closer. Faster. Someone working by hand."

The older figure folded their arms.

"A scav?"

"Not if your watch is competent."

That bought him the room's displeasure in full.

He let it stand.

"A scav takes the lantern, the coat, the knife, and anything else not tied down," he said. "They don't remove one person cleanly from a boundary post and leave the route marks behind."

The runner by the door spoke again.

"Marks weren't untouched."

Everyone turned.

"One of the cuts at the runoff lip had been deepened after the fact," they said. "Not enough to change the route. Enough to show someone had put a blade to it."

Cassian looked at the torn strip of cloth.

"Show me."

The runner stepped forward and pointed to the third mark in the sequence. Fresh bright thread showed where the cut had been reopened.

Not random.

Not damage.

Attention.

Thena saw it at the same time he did.

"Someone wanted the sign noticed."

"Yes," Cassian said.

The older figure's voice flattened further.

"By us?"

"By whoever came looking next."

The room went still again.

The lantern on the cabinet hissed softly. Somewhere outside the interrogation room, a pipe knocked three times in the wall and fell quiet.

The older figure said, "You're implying intent."

"I'm stating it."

"Toward who?"

Cassian let his gaze rest on the altered route mark.

"Me."

Thena muttered, "There it is."

The masked watcher lowered the weapon by a fraction.

"The hunter."

Cassian did not look at them.

"Maybe."

"That's not good enough," the older figure said.

"It's what I have until I see the ground."

"And if it is your hunter?"

Cassian thought of Gor's shoulder driving through him like a remembered lesson. The rooted stance. The disdain for waste. The habit of turning contact into judgment.

"Then he didn't come here because he needed your routes," Cassian said. "He came here because he knew I would choose them."

The older figure absorbed that without visible reaction.

"Meaning?"

"Meaning Hive pursuit follows systems. He follows decisions."

Thena gave a short, humorless laugh.

"I liked the machine better."

No one else did.

The older figure pulled out the last empty chair and sat for the first time since they entered.

The choice changed the room more than a raised voice would have.

"There are rules here," they said. "No open channels. No live mesh. No one crosses a marked line without being seen twice. No one gets shelter for free."

Thena said, "We noticed."

"Good."

They looked at Cassian.

"If one of ours was taken because your shadow crossed our boundary, that debt is yours before it is mine."

"Understood," Cassian said.

"No," the older figure said. "Not understood. Accepted."

Cassian held their gaze.

"Accepted."

The runner at the rear door shifted their weight.

"You're taking him out?"

"Not alone," the older figure said.

Thena's head came up immediately.

"He's not going anywhere without me."

"Your hand says otherwise."

"My hand isn't walking."

The masked watcher almost smiled.

Almost.

The older figure considered Thena for a beat, then dismissed the argument with a glance.

"You'll both go," they said. "Under watch. You read the ground. You do not lead the route. You do not touch our markers unless told. You do not speak to anyone on perimeter watch unless asked directly."

Thena leaned back again.

"That sounded almost like hospitality."

"It wasn't."

The older figure slid the torn route cloth across the table toward Cassian.

"If this mark was for you, read it."

Cassian looked down.

Three standard cuts.

One reopened.

Not enough to alter meaning.

Enough to alter emphasis.

A correction, not to the path but to the eye.

Look here.

He said, "It's not a message. It's proof."

"Proof of what?"

"That he was at the line. Close enough to handle one of your watchers, inspect the mark system, and leave it usable on purpose."

The older figure's face did not change.

The room did.

The masked watcher's weapon came fully down now, not out of trust but out of reprioritization. The runner by the door took one step back toward the corridor, already half-turned toward the work ahead.

Thena looked at Cassian.

"You can hear how bad that sounds, right?"

"Yes."

"Good."

Another knock came from outside.

Single.

Then two in quick succession.

The older figure answered with two taps on the table edge.

Protocol again.

Permission moving through the room by hand.

A voice from the corridor said, "Outer lantern on runoff cut just went dark."

No one in the room moved for half a second.

Then everyone moved at once.

The masked watcher checked the weapon.

The runner took the coat and broken blade.

Thena stood, jaw tightening as pain hit her hand and was ignored.

The older figure remained seated one second longer, eyes on Cassian.

"Terms of shelter," they said. "You help us find ours. We decide after that whether you still have any."

Cassian rose.

"Fair."

"No," the older figure said. "Necessary."

They stood.

At the door, they paused just long enough to add one more rule.

"If you see your hunter," they said, "you say so before you try to settle something older than this place."

Cassian did not answer immediately.

The warning was too accurate to dismiss.

"Understood," he said.

The older figure opened the door.

Cold damp air moved in from the corridor, carrying runoff, old stone, and the faint metal scent of lights running on strained current.

As they stepped out, Cassian looked once more at the reopened route mark in the runner's hand.

Not a message.

Not a threat.

Recognition.

Gor knew he was below the map.

Now the question was how much of the dark he had already learned.

Chapter 5 – The Opening Move
Korin didn’t move as Cassian stepped into the room. He simply turned his body slightly, making space—as if they were both guests at a private gathering no one had been invited to.
Cassian said nothing. He stood just inside the door, weight evenly distributed, shoulders loose, gaze steady.
Korin’s voice was warm. Polished. A practiced neutrality draped in charm.
"It’s good to see you up, Mr. Rho. You’re recovering faster than projected."
Cassian didn’t answer immediately. He watched the man’s eyes—too still, too focused. The kind of stillness that didn’t blink unless it served a purpose.
“Am I?” he finally said.
Korin smiled, just enough.
“Physically, at least. Cognitive function appears… formidable.”
Cassian shifted slightly. “You’re not with medical.”
“Observant,” Korin replied. “I oversee cognitive infrastructure and stability analysis across Central Hive operations. It was inevitable we’d speak.”
The words sounded official. So did the posture. But there was something behind them—not a lie, but a mask.
“You’ve read my file,” Cassian said flatly.
“Such as it exists,” Korin said, folding his hands before him. “You’ve been gone quite a long time. The data from your mission was… fragmented.”
Cassian gave a faint, humorless smile. “Convenient.”
Korin nodded, unoffended. “Indeed. And curious.”
Cassian moved closer to the bed, sat on the edge, never turning his back.
“You’re not here to welcome me home,” he said.
“No,” Korin agreed easily. “Though it is a kind of homecoming, isn’t it? The return of a long-lost explorer. A classic motif.”
Cassian narrowed his eyes. The man was testing him.
“So what is this?” Cassian asked. “Evaluation? Interrogation?”
Korin tilted his head, smiling faintly.
“Conversation.”
Cassian didn’t believe that for a second. Every word was calibrated. Every pause, every expression—a move.
He leaned forward slightly. “Your tone’s too gentle. That tells me you’re hiding the sharp end of the blade.”
Korin’s expression didn’t change. “And you haven’t asked a single question about the world you’ve returned to. That tells me you already don’t trust what you’ll hear.”
They locked eyes.
Neither blinked.
A board had been placed between them.
And the first move had just been made.
---
Korin finally broke the silence. “Very well,” he said smoothly. “Let’s put some cards on the table.”
He took a slow step toward the room’s terminal, waving a hand to activate a subdued schematic that projected mid-air.
“You are in Hive Spindle 9. Southern hemisphere. Earth is no longer a single geopolitical entity, but rather a unified cognitive mesh governed by layered consensus and infrastructure. The current cycle is standard year 2416. You’ve been missing for just over four centuries.”
Cassian didn’t respond. His posture remained still, but his jaw tensed slightly.
Korin tapped the display again.
“You were found… here.” He highlighted a location—a debris field in the black edge of the Helios Drift. “Your vessel was embedded in the shattered frame of what appears to be a Helion-class scoutcraft.
No active systems. Hull breach across three quadrants. No munitions. No fuel.
By all available data, you should not have survived.”
Cassian’s eyes flicked to the display. He said nothing.
Korin allowed a slight pause.
“You were unconscious, still strapped into the pilot’s harness, pulse steady, neural baseline faint but intact. There was evidence of… extreme engagement. Tactical maneuvers consistent with evasion under high-stress conditions. The outer hull of your ship bore impact signatures we haven’t seen in any known conflict.”
Still, Cassian didn’t speak. His expression hadn’t changed—but Korin noticed the slight shift in breathing, the way his fingers tightened on his thigh.
Korin gave a soft smile, deliberate.
“Your immediate priority, Mr. Rho, is recovery. Physical rehabilitation. Cognitive orientation. Reintegration. Or as we call it—assimilation.”
Cassian’s eyes returned to Korin.
Now he spoke.
“I didn’t ask for that.”
“No,” Korin said calmly. “But you’re here now. And Hive policy does not discard what it can still make use of.”
Cassian sat back slightly. Thoughtful.
So. He was being studied. Contained. Absorbed.
But not yet controlled.
Korin didn’t press. He simply stood, hands still behind his back, gaze patient.
The next move was Cassian’s.
Korin made it easier.
“Tell me,” he said casually, as if asking about a dream. “Have you experienced any... disorientation? Hallucinations? Memories that feel disjointed or out of place?”
Cassian blinked.
The question was smooth. Too smooth.
Not ‘Are you confused?’ but Have you remembered something you shouldn’t have?
Cassian let a beat of silence stretch, then gave a faint shrug.
“Nothing useful,” he said. “Mostly static. Some light. Nothing coherent.”
A lie.
He remembered the cockpit. The wave. The pressure. The voice.
But Korin didn’t need to know that.
“Expected,” Korin replied, showing no reaction. “Temporal transit trauma often scrambles short-term recall. It will resolve.”
Cassian watched him carefully.
Korin wasn’t surprised.
Which meant either he believed him.
Or he didn’t.
And was waiting for the next crack.
Cassian sat back on the edge of the bed, posture loose, mind tightening.
He’d survived the jump.
But now the real game had begun.

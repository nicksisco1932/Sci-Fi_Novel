The bridge swayed once under their combined weight, then settled into a low metallic hum.
Cassian kept his pace even.
The reservoir below was too dark to read cleanly. Black water sat far beneath the broken catwalks, barely moving except where distant runoff struck it in thin silver lines. The guide lights ahead stayed narrow and exact, a single path drawn across open space with no effort to make it feel safe.
Thena stayed one step behind him.
"If this thing gives way," she said, "I'm blaming your invisible strategist."
"If it gives way," Cassian said, "blame the century it was neglected."
"That's less satisfying."
They crossed in silence after that.
Halfway over, Cassian stopped just long enough to listen.
The chamber carried old noise: water, current, expansion ticks in the metal. No immediate pursuit. No echo of heavy footfalls. The route had bought them time.
Not much.
He kept moving.
At the far end, the bridge met a suspended platform bolted into the wall of the reservoir chamber. A service door waited there under a dead inspection lamp, sealed so tightly it might have been fused shut.
It opened before either of them touched it.

Thena raised her blade.
"No."
"No what?" she asked.
"No sudden movements unless you're certain you can kill the room."
"You say useful things in terrible ways."
Cassian stepped through first.

The chamber beyond had once been a relay watch station. Enough remained to show its shape: control banks stripped to frames, broken monitors, a rack of manual override tools rusting in brackets by the wall. But someone had cleared the center of the room recently. A table. Two chairs. A med case. Three sealed water packs. Power routed through one active terminal at the far end.
Prepared.
Not comfortable. Deliberately not comfortable.

The door sealed behind them.
Four deadbolts turned in sequence.

Thena pivoted toward it at once.
"Still like your categories?"
"More now than before."
Cassian scanned the room.
No obvious weapon mounts.
No sedation ports in the ceiling.
No camera lenses he could see, though that meant very little.
The terminal brightened.

SIT

Thena laughed once, sharp and humorless.
"There it is."
Cassian did not sit.
"You first," she said.
"No."
"Then neither do I."

The text vanished.
New lines replaced it immediately:

THEN STAND
LISTEN
YOU WERE TRACKED FROM THE CLINIC

Thena's expression changed before she could stop it.
"What?"
Cassian was already moving toward her injured hand.
"Hold still."
"Cassian."
"Hold still."

He set her wrist under the lamp, angled the bandage, and looked past the clotting agent and dried blood to the outer wrap the clinic leader had pressed over it. Cheap gauze. Gray with grime.
Wrong texture.
He peeled one edge back.
A filament flashed inside the weave, hair-thin and silver.
Passive.
Low power.
Enough.

Thena went very still. "I want that out."
"I know."
He stripped the outer layer away, found the thread stitched through it in a grid too regular to be accidental, and lifted it free between two fingers. Warm.
The terminal updated:

BURN IT

Cassian crossed to the table, found a maintenance lighter in the cleared supplies, and touched flame to the filament. It curled black almost instantly, giving off a bitter chemical stink.
Thena looked at the smoke, then at the screen.
"I hate this person."
"That's rational."
"Do you know who we're talking to?"
"Not yet."

The med case clicked open on its own.
Fresh bandage rolls sat inside beside sealant, clotting foam, a pain suppressor ampule, and a narrow blade sterilized and ready.
Not scavenged.
Sorted.
Intentional.

Thena stared at the arrangement.
"This was staged."
"Yes."
"For us."
"Yes."
"You're taking this very calmly."
"No," Cassian said. "I'm separating useful fear from wasted fear."

He rewrapped her hand with the clean bandage, tighter and cleaner than before. Thena hissed once through her teeth but let him work.
The screen stayed dark while he finished.
That almost bothered him more.

When he sealed the last turn of gauze, the terminal lit again.

HIVE TEAM PASSED ABOVE YOU
GOR DID NOT
YOU HAVE NINE MINUTES

Thena looked up sharply. "It knows where he is too?"
"It knows enough."
"That's not better."
"No."

She flexed her hand once, testing the wrap, then reached for one of the water packs without taking her eyes off the terminal.
"All right," she said. "Questions."
"Start with useful ones."
"I was going to."

She stepped toward the screen.
"Who are you?"

Nothing.

"Fine," she said. "What do you want with him?"

The reply came fast enough to feel impatient.

SURVIVAL
THEN ACCURACY

Thena glanced at Cassian. "I liked it better when it was pretending not to have a personality."
"It wasn't pretending."

Cassian moved closer to the terminal now.
The font was blunt. Functional. No ornamental styling, no attempt at reassurance. It reminded him of nothing in Hive presentation logic. Too direct. Too willing to discard anything that was not the point.
He said, "What does accuracy mean?"

The screen held for a beat.

WHAT DID ALYEN REACH

He said nothing.

The question came again, refined:

WHAT MEMORY SURFACED

Thena looked between him and the screen.
"You don't have to answer that."
"I know."
"Do you trust it?"
"No."
"Then why are you still reading?"
"Because it hasn't been wrong yet."

The words sat in the room.
Not because they were reassuring.
Because they weren't.

Another line appeared:

YOU HEARD SOMETHING BEFORE IMPACT

Cassian felt the old pressure move behind his eyes.
Cockpit glass.
The wave.
The warning landing where language usually formed.

Stop the expansion.

He looked away from the terminal for a moment and centered his breathing.
Thena read the shift in him.
"Cassian."
"I'm fine."
"That's not what your face says."
"My face is not the priority."

The screen changed again.

ANSWER
OR LEAVE

Thena's grip tightened on the water pack.
"I really hate this person."
"That also remains rational."

Cassian stepped closer until the glow from the terminal flattened the planes of his face and threw the rest of the room into shadow.
He could leave.
Take the supplies. Take the route. Accept the help and refuse the exchange.
But refusal was also data.
Whoever had arranged the bridge, the relay chamber, the staged medical kit, and the timed warning already knew how he moved under pressure. Silence would not preserve anything except ignorance.

He said, "A phrase."

The screen stayed blank for half a second.

STATE IT

He did not like how quickly the room seemed to narrow.
Thena took a half step toward him. Not to stop him. To be close enough if the answer mattered in a way neither of them liked.

"Stop the expansion," Cassian said.

For the first time since the terminal woke, the response lagged.
One second.
Two.

Then:

GOOD
YOU HEARD IT CLEAN

Cassian's eyes hardened.
"You knew it was there."

No answer.

He leaned one hand against the table, ribs protesting the shift.
"What was it?"

Nothing.

Then, slowly:

NOT WHAT
WHO

Thena went silent.
Cassian did too.

The room seemed to contract around the single word.
Not what.
Who.

The terminal printed one final block of text:

TAKE THE WEST DESCENT
AVOID OPEN CHANNELS
DO NOT LET GOR FORCE CONTACT RANGE
I WILL OPEN THE NEXT DOOR

Below it, after a pause:

WHEN YOU REMEMBER MORE
TELL ME EXACTLY

The screen went black.
No fade.
No transition.
Gone.

Thena let out a breath she had been holding too long.
"That was not normal."
"No."
"Was that your Reversionist?"
"Maybe."
"You sound unconvinced."
"I am unconvinced."
"By what?"

Cassian looked at the dead terminal, at the med case, at the room that had been prepared to keep them alive only long enough to keep them moving.
"By how much it already knows," he said.

The far wall unlocked with a heavy mechanical clunk.
A narrow door slid open on the west side of the chamber, revealing a descending corridor lit by the same thin white guide strips as the bridge.

Thena picked up the med case.
"I assume we're obeying the haunted infrastructure."
"For now."
"You make terrible plans."
"No," Cassian said. "I make plans that survive contact."

She gave him a dry look, then moved to the new doorway.
Cassian stayed one second longer in front of the dark screen.
Not what.
Who.

The phrase refused to leave him alone.
He turned and followed Thena down the west descent before the room could change its mind.

Chapter 14 – The Broken Clinic
They did not stop until the attacker's voice had faded and the tunnels had swallowed the last of the rain. Cassian slowed only when Thena stumbled, breath turning ragged as blood worked through the puncture in her hand.
The off-grid levels were darker here. Whole runs of light had failed. The access corridor sloped downward through mold, coolant stink, and old rust.
"We're close," Thena said.
Cassian kept a steadying hand at her elbow. "Close to what?"
She nodded toward a scav mark cut into the wall: three hard lines, old and deliberate.
"Clinic."
Not Hive. Not regulated. Not safe.
But it was the only place nearby with any chance of keeping the wound clean enough to use.
A crooked doorway sagged ahead, lit by a lantern that pulsed instead of burned. Cassian caught the shapes inside before the smell reached him: thin bodies on crates, a few cots against the wall, someone groaning through fever.
Then the air hit.
Rot under cheap disinfectant. Old coolant. Untreated wounds.
This was not care.
It was triage with a roof.
A man by the entrance looked up, eyes too sharp for someone pretending to rest. "How bad?"
Cassian kept his voice flat. "Clotting agent. Bandage. Nothing complicated."
The man's gaze slid to Thena and lingered there for the wrong reasons.
Cassian moved half a step and blocked it.
"We don't need a bed."
That was when the leader came out from the back.
Broad frame. Greasy work jacket. Forearms built by labor, not discipline. His expression stayed bored until he saw Thena. Then it changed into something evaluating.
"I can help," he said. "For a price."
"We'll pay," Cassian said.
The man set a battered med-kit on a metal table. QuikClot. A half-clean roll of gauze. A syringe Cassian would not use unless forced. He took Thena's wrist without asking.
Cassian shifted on instinct.
The man's grip paused just long enough to acknowledge the warning.
"Relax," he muttered. "You brought her here."
He dusted the wound with clotting agent. Thena sucked air through her teeth and locked her free hand around the table edge until the knuckles blanched.
The bleeding slowed.
The man stepped closer instead of stepping back. Too close. His breath smelled of cheap alcohol and older violence.
"You know," he said, "a woman like this shouldn't be moving these grids without someone looking after her."
Cassian's voice went flat. "Bind the hand."
The man smirked. "She's safe here if she's smart."
The threat sat in the room even before anyone answered it.
Cassian closed the distance in a single step. No display. No raised voice. Just intent.
The clinic went quiet.
"Bind her hand," Cassian said again. "Nothing else."
For a moment he thought the man would test it, if only to prove this room answered to him.
Then the smirk slipped.
"Fine," he said. "You're no fun at all."
Cassian wrapped Thena's hand himself once the supplies were on the table, tight enough to hold, loose enough to preserve movement.
"We're done," he said.
They turned for the door.
The guard near the entrance shifted into their path for half a second, weighing injury against leverage. Cassian met his eyes and held them.
The guard moved.
Cool damp air met them outside.
Thena exhaled only when the clinic was two turns behind them. "That wasn't a clinic."
"No," Cassian said. "It was a trap that stocked bandages."
She looked at him. "You saw it before I did."
"I've seen the type before," he said. "Places where need becomes currency and someone decides to call that order."
"And people live with it?"
"People live with whatever keeps them alive until morning."
They kept moving. Water ticked somewhere inside the walls. Shadows broke and reformed around them.
The attacker felt farther away now, but not gone. Men like that did not lose interest. They banked it.
"Cassian," Thena said, reading his face, "he's not giving up, is he?"
"No," Cassian said. "He's giving us room to think we're ahead."
They went deeper into the maze, carrying the smallest possible version of safety with them.
Down here the Hive had no visible hand.
Only absence.
And absence had grown its own predators.

The transit shell ran blind for so long that time stopped feeling measurable.
There were no windows, no status lights, no guide strips waking in sequence to tell them the next step had already been decided. Just the hard rattle of steel wheels under the cab and the occasional shift in pressure when the buried rail curved through spaces too large to guess at.

Thena sat opposite Cassian with the med case between her boots.
"I liked the part where the machine lied to us in complete sentences," she said.
"You still have me."
"That's not the same kind of problem."

The shell jolted, dropped a fraction, then leveled out again.
Cassian kept one hand against the wall and counted the movement the way he would count breaths in a fight. Turn. Grade change. Short straight. Another turn. The map in his head stayed skeletal. Too many missing anchors. Too much dark between decisions.

That bothered him.
It also mattered.
Without the hidden route logic feeding them doors and delays, they were back inside ordinary uncertainty.

The shell slowed.

No message warned them.
No light changed.
It just began to lose momentum until the wheels screamed once against the rail and the cab stopped hard enough to push the med case sideways across the floor.

For a second nothing happened.
Then the front panel dropped open.

Black air waited beyond it.
Not empty.
Cold. Wet. Mineral.

Thena peered past the opening and made a face.
"Tell me this looks navigable to you."
"No."
"Good."

Cassian climbed out first.

The shell had stopped on a maintenance spur ending in open dark. A narrow service lip ran along one wall. Below it, nothing visible. Above, a ceiling lost in shadow. The only light came from the shell itself, a dim service lamp over the hatch that reached just far enough to prove the place had scale and not far enough to define it.

He crouched and touched the rail.
Cold.
No residual hum.
Whatever had carried them here had spent the last of its useful life on the trip.

Thena handed him the med case and climbed out after him.
"Do you hear that?"

He did.
Not machinery.
Water, but not the controlled circulation of pumpworks. Open flow. Slow and broad, moving somewhere below them through stone or old concrete with no system left to discipline it.

She looked back into the shell.
"Nothing?"

The dark screen inside reflected only the weak hatch light.
No last instruction.
No apology.
No path.

"Nothing," Cassian said.
"Intermittent," Thena muttered. "Right."

The lip ended ten meters ahead at a bolted ladder descending into shadow. No indicator lit it. No message claimed it.
On the wall beside the top rung, someone had carved three short diagonal cuts into the metal.
Not machine-clean.
Done by hand.
Old enough to have collected grime. Recent enough that the edges still held shape.

Thena stepped closer.
"That's not Hive."

"I know."

She ran her thumb just above the cuts without touching them.
"I've seen marks like this in old lane routes. Not here. Higher up. Hand-offs. Safe passage if you know whose pattern you're looking at."

"Do you know this pattern?"

She gave him a flat look.
"If I did, I wouldn't be staring at it."

Cassian studied the wall around the mark.
No second sign. No directional code he recognized. Just the ladder, the dark below it, and the absence of alternatives.

He started down.

The metal was slick with condensation and older rust. The ladder descended farther than it should have, past cross-braced concrete, past sealed conduit mouths, past one collapsed landing half swallowed by mineral buildup. By the time his boots touched solid ground, the shell above was already only a dull square of light.

The chamber at the bottom was not a chamber at all.
It was a buried street.

Or something that had once tried to be one.

The ceiling arched high enough to suggest civic intent, then vanished into fracture and hanging dark. Two parallel walkways ran above a central channel of black water moving slow between retaining walls. On either side, recessed doorframes and shuttered service fronts followed the curve away into distance. A city concept, translated underground, then abandoned before anyone had finished pretending it made sense.

Thena reached the floor beside him and stood still.
"All right," she said quietly. "I officially do not know this part."

Cassian turned once in place, reading what he could.
No live terminals.
No mesh glow.
No active relay nodes humming behind the walls.
The air itself felt outside the map, not because it was wild but because nothing here was still talking to anything else.

He said, "Below the map."

Thena looked at him. "What?"

"That's what it called this."

She glanced up into the dark above them.
"Of course it did."

They moved along the left walkway because the right had partially collapsed into the water and because Cassian distrusted symmetry when someone else had last selected the route.
Their footsteps traveled too far.
Every sound came back altered by space.

At the first recessed storefront, the metal shutter had been forced halfway open long ago. Inside sat a room full of broken shelving, flood stains, and the skeletal frame of a transport cradle too old for current Hive manufacture. Someone had scavenged it down to structure and left the shell.
Human absence, not machine failure.

Thena kept her voice low.
"If people use this place, they use it carefully."

"Or not often."

"Same difference down here."

They passed another hand-cut marker at the next junction.
Two vertical lines. One horizontal scored through them.
Then a third, farther on, this one almost worn away beneath mineral crust.

Not random.
Language.
Just not theirs.

Cassian stopped at the third marker and looked back the way they had come.

Thena noticed.
"What?"

"Nothing behind us yet."

"You say that like 'yet' already matters."

"It does."

He did not need a terminal to tell him the obvious.
Hive pursuit lost efficiency without system assistance.
Gor did not.

The hunter did not need doors opened.
He did not need floodgates tripped for him or channels blinded in advance.
All he needed was the line of Cassian's thinking.

Thena read enough of his face to follow the direction of it.
"You think he'll come here too."

"If he decides I would."

"Would you?"

Cassian looked down the buried street, at the dead fronts and black water and human marks cut by hands that expected no algorithm to rescue them.
"Yes," he said.

"That's annoying."

"Yes."

They reached the first place where the walkway widened into a plaza shape. Once there might have been light wells or kiosks or some civic fiction about what people were meant to do there. Now the space held only debris islands, hanging cable roots, and an old map board standing crooked on one support.

Thena saw it first and gave a dry, exhausted laugh.
"Please tell me the haunted machine left us one joke."

Cassian crossed to the board.
Its surface had been layered over so many times the original print no longer mattered. Old route colors showed faintly under soot and grime, but later hands had cut across them with charcoal, paint, knife scratches, and adhesive tags long since peeled away.
Maps over maps over refusal.

Near the center, one phrase had been written by hand in block letters thick enough to survive damp:

NO UPLINK BELOW THIS LINE

Under it, in smaller script:

GOOD

Thena read it over his shoulder.
"I like whoever wrote that."

"You like anyone who insults the Hive with decent timing."

"True."

He traced the lower edge of the board without touching it.
Most of the hand-drawn paths were illegible now, but one sequence of marks repeated from the ladder wall to the walkway junctions to a route sketched faintly toward the far end of the buried street.
Three diagonals.
Pause.
Two verticals crossed once.

Pattern.

"There," he said.

Thena narrowed her eyes. "You can actually read that?"

"Not read. Infer."

"Sometimes I forget how irritating that is."

They followed the marks deeper.
No terminals woke for them. No hidden text arrived to confirm the choice. That absence altered the pace more than either of them admitted. Every doorway had to be judged on sight. Every turn held equal weight until they made it costly by choosing.

At the far end of the street, the water channel widened and disappeared through a breach in the retaining wall. The walkway broke with it, forcing them into a service corridor cut through the concrete by later hands. Rough work. Uneven edges. Not built by the Hive. Rebuilt against it.

Thena touched the wall.
"This wasn't supposed to be here."

"No."

"People made it anyway."

"Yes."

That seemed to steady her more than the hidden route logic had.
Human work.
Visible work.
Something built by hands that might fail for ordinary reasons.

They ducked into the corridor.
The air changed immediately. Less open water. More dust. More old habitation buried under damp and time. Cassian caught the smell of extinguished fire, stale fabric, and metal that had been heated and reshaped recently enough to remember the process.

Occupied once.
Maybe occupied now.

Thena heard it a second later: not words, just the faintest clatter somewhere ahead, followed by abrupt stillness.

She drew her blade.
"Not alone."

Cassian set the med case down without taking his eyes off the dark.
"No."

"Friendly?"

"We have no basis for that."

"You're a pleasure in confined spaces."

He ignored that and listened.
Not pursuit rhythm.
Too light.
Too irregular.
Not Hive boots.
Not Gor.

That did not make it safe.

On the wall beside the corridor mouth, almost hidden under soot, another hand mark waited.
Not the route language this time.
A single line of paint, thumb-width, dark red once and now flaked nearly brown.
Fresh enough to matter.

Stop line.
Boundary.
Warning.

Thena saw it and lowered her voice further.
"We step over that and we're announcing ourselves."

"We're already announced."

"I know. I preferred pretending otherwise."

From deeper in the corridor came a small metallic knock.
Then another.
Measured.
Not accidental.

Signal.

Cassian and Thena looked at each other once.
No infrastructure. No hidden terminal. No machine choosing for them.
Just the line on the wall, the dark ahead, and whoever had decided to answer their arrival with something human enough to make sound by hand.

Cassian picked up the med case.
"We go carefully."

Thena adjusted her grip on the blade.
"You always say that right before things become terrible."

"Then our expectations are aligned."

Together, they stepped over the line and into the part of the dark the map had failed to keep.
